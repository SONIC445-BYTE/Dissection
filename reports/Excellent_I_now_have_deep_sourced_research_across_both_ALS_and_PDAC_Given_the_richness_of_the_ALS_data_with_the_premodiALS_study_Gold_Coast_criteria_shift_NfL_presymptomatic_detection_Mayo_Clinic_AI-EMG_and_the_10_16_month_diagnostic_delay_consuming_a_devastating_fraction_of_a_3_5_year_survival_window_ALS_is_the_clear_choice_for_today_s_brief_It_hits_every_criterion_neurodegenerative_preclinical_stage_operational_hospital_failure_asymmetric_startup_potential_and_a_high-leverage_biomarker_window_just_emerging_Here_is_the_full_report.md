## Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) — 2026-07-27

---

### 1) Snapshot (one line)

**Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)** — why early diagnosis fails in practice: A 10–16 month diagnostic delay, caused by non-specific early motor symptoms, rigid multi-region diagnostic criteria (El Escorial), and systematic misdirection of patients through non-neurology specialties, consumes up to **40% of a patient's entire survival window** before a confirmed diagnosis is reached.

---

### 2) Why Early Diagnosis Fails (5 bullets)

- **Biological barrier:** ALS presents initially with focal, unilateral weakness, fasciculations, or subtle speech changes — symptoms indistinguishable from cervical myelopathy, myasthenia gravis, multifocal motor neuropathy, or even normal aging. No single early symptom is pathognomonic.
- **Criteria limitation (El Escorial):** The revised El Escorial criteria require evidence of *both* upper and lower motor neuron (UMN + LMN) degeneration across *multiple anatomical regions* (bulbar, cervical, thoracic, lumbar). In early ALS, patients typically only have signs in one region — meaning strict adherence excludes up to **60% of early ALS patients** from a definitive diagnosis and clinical trial enrollment until disease is advanced.
- **Test limitation:** EMG (the gold standard) detects LMN damage but requires a skilled neurophysiologist to interpret subtle early changes. The new Gold Coast criteria (2022, Turner et al.) improve sensitivity by requiring only one region with LMN evidence + progressive weakness — but adoption across hospitals remains inconsistent.
- **Biomarker gap:** Neurofilament light chain (NfL) — the most validated fluid biomarker — is elevated in plasma **up to 2 years before clinical diagnosis** (UK Biobank data, premodiALS 2025), yet is still not routinely ordered in primary care or even general neurology. NfL is also non-specific (rises in MS, TBI, Alzheimer's), requiring disease-context interpretation.
- **System failure:** Real-world AI analysis of Spain's SESCAM Healthcare Network EHRs (2025) found that **88.4% of pre-diagnosis ALS patients were seen in primary care** and **38.4% in the emergency room** before ever reaching a neurologist. Patients with respiratory-onset ALS (presenting with dyspnea) face the *longest delays* — typically 12+ months — as they are routed to pulmonology and cardiology first.

---

### 3) Detection Window & Gap

| Stage | Signal | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Earliest detectable (research/ideal)** | Elevated plasma NfL (SiMoA assay); presymptomatic in familial ALS gene carriers | **~24 months before clinical diagnosis** |
| **Preclinical functional change** | Subtle EMG abnormalities in clinically normal muscles; microRNA panel (98% accuracy, Brain Chemistry Labs/Hopkins) | **6–12 months before symptom onset** |
| **Typical clinical detection** | Confirmed diagnosis after EMG, clinical exam, mimic rule-out | **10–16 months after first symptom** |
| **Gap to close** | **~24–36 months** total from earliest detectable signal to current confirmed diagnosis |

**Practical impact of the gap:** Median survival from symptom onset is 3–5 years. A 12-month diagnostic delay means patients lose **20–33% of their survival window** before treatment begins. Earlier diagnosis enables riluzole (the only FDA-approved disease-modifying therapy) and clinical trial enrollment at a stage where motor neuron reserve is still meaningful.

---

### 4) What's Being Used Today (Gold Standard + Emergent)

**Gold Standards:**
- **Electromyography (EMG):** Detects LMN damage in clinically affected and clinically silent muscles. Remains irreplaceable but operator-dependent and under-utilized in early stages.
- **Clinical examination (UMN + LMN signs):** Progressive weakness pattern mapped against El Escorial / Gold Coast criteria. Gold Coast criteria (Turner et al., *JNNP* 2022) are more sensitive and are being increasingly adopted.
- **MRI brain/spine:** Rules out structural mimics (cervical myelopathy, cord compression).
- **Nerve conduction studies (NCS):** Differentiates from peripheral neuropathies.

**Emerging Research / Tools:**
- **Plasma NfL (SiMoA platform):** Elevated 2 years pre-diagnosis; validated in UK Biobank; primary biomarker in premodiALS study. Tracks disease progression and therapy response. *Not yet standard of care.*
- **MicroRNA blood panel (Brain Chemistry Labs / Johns Hopkins, 2024):** Reported 98% accuracy in differentiating ALS from mimics. Rapid, blood-based, potentially deployable in general neurology clinics.
- **AI-enhanced EMG (Mayo Clinic, Dr. Nathan Staff):** Machine learning trained on 45,000+ EMGs detects subtle LMN patterns in F-wave responses invisible to human interpretation. Reduces time-to-EMG-confirmation.
- **Multi-omic presymptomatic signature (premodiALS study, Tzeplaeff et al., 2025):** Longitudinal multi-center study (n=330, 6 sites, 218 enrolled as of June 2025) profiling proteomics, metabolomics, lipidomics across plasma, serum, CSF, urine, and **tear fluid** in presymptomatic gene carriers vs. early symptomatic vs. healthy controls. Goal: individual-level ML model predicting phenoconversion timing.
- **EHR-NLP flagging (SESCAM AI study, 2025):** NLP applied to primary care notes to detect hidden ALS symptom clusters (progressive weakness + fasciculations + dysphagia) and trigger early neurology referral.

**Main Limitations:**
- NfL is non-specific (rises in multiple neurodegenerative diseases) — requires disease context
- MicroRNA panel not yet FDA-cleared or commercially deployed at scale
- AI-EMG tools are institution-specific, not interoperable
- Multi-omic approaches remain research-stage; not deployable in routine clinic in 90 days

### 5) Where Healthcare Is Failing (Operational Insight)

**Screening point that drops the ball:**
Primary care — the first point of contact for 88.4% of ALS patients — has no structured ALS screening protocol, no NfL reflex ordering pathway, and no symptom-cluster alert in most EHR systems. A patient presenting with progressive hand weakness + fatigue will receive a metabolic panel and rheumatology referral before anyone thinks "neurology."

**Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:**
**Primary care → neurology referral pathway redesign.** Implementing a simple EHR-embedded alert rule (progressive limb weakness + fasciculations + no clear orthopedic/rheumatologic cause → mandatory neurology referral within 2 weeks) costs near-zero and could shave 3–6 months off the diagnostic delay. Cleveland Clinic survey data confirms that long specialist wait times and lack of primary care ALS awareness are the top two fixable causes of delay.

**High-risk population missed:**
1. **Respiratory-onset ALS patients** (dyspnea as first symptom) — routed to pulmonology, cardiac workup, and COPD management for months before ALS is considered. Median delay: 12+ months.
2. **Bulbar-onset patients** (dysarthria/dysphagia first) — sent to ENT, speech therapy, or stroke neurology. Often misdiagnosed with stroke sequelae or functional neurological disorder.
3. **Older patients with comorbidities** — progressive weakness attributed to hypertension, diabetes neuropathy, or "normal aging," delaying neuromuscular specialist referral.
4. **Familial ALS gene carriers (SOD1, C9orf72, FUS)** — presymptomatic individuals who could benefit most from NfL monitoring are not enrolled in any structured surveillance program outside of research centers.

### 6) 3 High-Leverage Solution Ideas (Practical, Ranked)

**🥇 Idea A — EHR Symptom-Cluster Alert + Reflex Neurology Referral (30-day pilot)**
*What:* Deploy a rule-based EHR alert in a primary care network: if a patient aged 40–75 has ≥2 visits in 6 months documenting progressive limb weakness, muscle cramps/fasciculations, or unexplained dysphagia — trigger a mandatory neurology referral order within 14 days.
*How to run the pilot:*
- Partner with 3–5 primary care practices in a hospital system (target: academic medical center with integrated EHR like Epic or Cerner)
- Build the alert rule in 2–4 weeks (no new software needed — Epic's BPA alert system)
- Run for 90 days; collect: (a) number of alerts fired, (b) referral completion rate, (c) time from alert to neurology appointment, (d) ALS diagnoses made within 6 months of flagged patients
- *Success metric:* ≥30% reduction in time from first symptom documentation to neurology referral vs. historical baseline

**🥈 Idea B — Plasma NfL Reflex Testing Protocol in Neuromuscular Clinics (60-day pilot)**
*What:* Implement NfL (SiMoA) as a reflex add-on test for any patient presenting to a neuromuscular clinic with progressive motor symptoms of unclear etiology. Use NfL elevation + clinical context to prioritize EMG scheduling and expedite diagnostic workup.
*Resource checklist:*
- SiMoA HD-X analyzer (or send-out lab to Quanterix-certified reference lab) — ~$150–200/test
- Clinical decision support protocol: NfL >10 pg/mL in age-adjusted context → expedited EMG within 2 weeks
- Retrospective chart review of 50 confirmed ALS cases to validate local NfL threshold
- Prospective tracking of NfL-ordered vs. non-ordered cohorts over 60 days
*Expected impact:* 2–4 month reduction in time from neuromuscular clinic first visit to EMG confirmation; earlier enrollment in clinical trials

**🥉 Idea C — Familial ALS Presymptomatic Surveillance Registry (Research/Startup)**
*What:* Build a longitudinal biomarker surveillance program for known familial ALS gene carriers (SOD1, C9orf72, FUS, TDP-43). Quarterly plasma NfL + annual multi-omic panel (aligned with premodiALS protocol) + digital motor function tracking (grip strength, gait analysis via wearable) to model phenoconversion timing.
*Highest upside:* This is the exact population where early intervention is actionable — catching the disease 12–24 months before symptom onset allows trial of neuroprotective therapies (antisense oligonucleotides for SOD1-ALS already exist: tofersen/Qalsody, FDA-approved 2023).
*Tests needed:*
- Validate individual NfL trajectory modeling (rate-of-rise > absolute level)
- Integrate digital biomarkers (speech acoustics, finger-tapping speed, wearable gait)
- Collaborate with: premodiALS consortium, Target ALS, Prize4Life, ALS Association
*Startup angle:* A SaaS platform for familial ALS surveillance + phenoconversion prediction has a clear payer pathway (gene therapy trial sponsors, pharma companies running ASO trials need pre-symptomatic cohorts)

### 7) First-Principles Signal Hunt (What We Should Measure Earlier)

**Hidden signal candidate:**
**Tear fluid NfL + neurofilament heavy chain (NfH)** — the premodiALS study is actively profiling tear fluid as a non-invasive, CNS-proximal biofluid. Tear fluid contains neurofilament proteins shed from corneal nerve fibers (which are affected in ALS), and collection is completely non-invasive (Schirmer strips). If validated, this could be a community-deployable, stigma-free screening tool requiring no phlebotomy.

Secondary signal: **Speech acoustic biomarkers** — subtle changes in articulation rate, vowel formant trajectories, and voice tremor are detectable by smartphone microphone analysis months before clinical dysarthria diagnosis. Multiple groups (including MIT and Columbia) are developing passive speech monitoring apps. In bulbar-onset ALS, this could be the earliest detectable signal.

**Minimal sampling change needed:**
- Plasma NfL: standard venipuncture → existing blood draw infrastructure, just needs test order + SiMoA-certified lab
- Tear fluid: Schirmer strip collection (30 seconds, no needle) → processable via mass spectrometry proteomics
- Speech: passive smartphone app → zero clinical infrastructure change

### 10) Pattern Insight (Meta-Learning)

**Recurring diagnostic failure pattern emerging:**

This is now the **third major pattern reinforcement** across the brief series: **the "criteria-biology mismatch" trap.**

The pattern: A disease has a *biological signal* that precedes clinical detectability by 1–3 years (NfL in ALS, CA19-9 in PDAC, cfDNA in early cancers). But the *diagnostic criteria* were designed around late-stage, confirmable disease states — not early biological signals. The system waits for the disease to prove itself across multiple organ systems or anatomical regions before acting, by which point the intervention window has narrowed dramatically.

**What's generalizable:**
- In **ALS**: El Escorial requires multi-region degeneration → biology (NfL) says "act 2 years earlier"
- In **PDAC**: CA19-9 cutoff (>37 U/mL) is calibrated for symptomatic disease → biology (THBS2+CA19-9 panel, urine LYVE1/REG1B/TFF1) says "act 2 years earlier"
- In **Ovarian cancer**: CA-125 fixed cutoff misses early-stage → ROCA (dynamic trajectory) says "the rate of change matters, not the absolute number"

**The generalizable opportunity forming:**
> **Replace static threshold diagnostics with dynamic trajectory biomarkers + AI-assisted pattern recognition at the primary care interface.** Every disease in this series has a "pre-threshold" biological signal window of 12–36 months that is systematically ignored because the clinical system is organized around *confirmation* rather than *suspicion*. The startup/hospital opportunity is not a new biomarker — it's a **triage intelligence layer** at primary care that converts subtle multi-signal patterns (NfL trajectory + symptom cluster + demographic risk) into actionable referral decisions, 12–18 months earlier than today's system.

**Breaking or reinforcing?** Strongly **reinforcing** — and now with enough repetition to call it a **platform thesis**: the next generation of early diagnosis is not a single better test, it is a *signal aggregation + dynamic monitoring* infrastructure sitting between primary care and specialist confirmation.
