Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — High-Grade Serous Ovarian Cancer (HGSOC) — 2026-04-10

**1) Snapshot (one line)**
High-Grade Serous Ovarian Cancer (HGSOC) — why early diagnosis fails in practice: The tumor originates in the fallopian tubes and sheds proximally into the closed peritoneal cavity for years, evading blood-based detection while vague GI symptoms misdirect patients to gastroenterologists.

**2) Why early diagnosis fails (3–5 bullets)**
*   **Biological barrier:** HGSOC begins as Serous Tubal Intraepithelial Carcinoma (STIC) lesions that shed into the peritoneal "sink" rather than the vascular system, keeping blood circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) loads functionally undetectable until late-stage metastasis.
*   **Test limitation:** The standard CA-125 blood test and Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVUS) have abysmal positive predictive value (PPV) for early-stage disease, leading to false positives and unnecessary oophorectomies, or false reassurance.
*   **System failure:** Symptoms (bloating, early satiety, pelvic pressure) are highly non-specific. Primary care routinely triages these as IBS or age-related GI distress, delaying GYN-oncology referral by an average of 6–12 months.

**3) Detection window & gap (concise)**
*   **Earliest detectable signal (research / ideal):** STIC lesions and proximal shedding in the fallopian/uterine environment (5–7 years before widespread peritoneal dissemination).
*   **Typical clinical detection:** Stage III/IV (presentation of ascites, widespread metastasis).
*   **Gap to close:** 5–7 years. Closing this shifts the 5-year survival rate from <30% to >90%.

**4) What’s being used today (gold standard + emergent)**
*   **Gold standard(s):** CA-125 blood test + TVUS, followed by surgical biopsy. (No population screening is currently recommended due to high false-positive harm).
*   **Emerging research / tools:** Proximal liquid biopsies (uterine lavage, tampon-based extracellular transcriptome capture); Multi-omic blood tests targeting tumor marker glycolipids/gangliosides (e.g., AOA Dx's AKRIVIS GD); Extracellular Vesicle (EV) profiling (e.g., Mercy BioAnalytics).
*   **Main limitations:** Blood tests still fight the "dilution effect" of the peritoneal barrier; uterine lavage requires a specialized, uncomfortable GYN visit; AI-driven imaging struggles with the sheer lack of distinct morphological changes in early tubes.

**5) Where healthcare is failing (operational insight)**
*   **Screening point that drops the ball:** Primary care EMR triage. Women >45 presenting with persistent, new-onset bloating or pelvic pressure are sent down a multi-month GI diagnostic pathway (endoscopies, diet changes) before a pelvic workup is even considered.
*   **Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:** Lack of automated reflex testing. We can implement EMR logic that automatically flags persistent GI complaints in this demographic to trigger a baseline GYN workup.
*   **High-risk population missed:** Women with BRCA1/2 or Lynch syndrome mutations who decline or delay prophylactic salpingo-oophorectomy, who currently lack a reliable interim molecular monitoring tool.
