Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma (PDAC) — 2026-03-23

1) Snapshot (one line)
Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma (PDAC) — why early diagnosis fails in practice: The tumor grows silently behind a dense fibrotic shield, presenting with non-specific metabolic and GI symptoms that are routinely dismissed or treated in silos until vascular invasion occurs.

2) Why early diagnosis fails (3–5 bullets)
*   **Biological barrier:** PDAC is characterized by a dense desmoplastic stroma (fibrotic tissue) that restricts blood flow, severely limiting the shedding of circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) into the bloodstream during early stages.
*   **Test limitation:** The current biomarker, CA19-9, has low positive predictive value for screening, frequently yielding false positives from benign biliary obstruction and false negatives in Lewis-antigen-negative patients (~10% of population).
*   **System failure:** Fragmented care means the classic early triad—new-onset diabetes, subtle weight loss, and vague back/epigastric pain—is managed separately by primary care, endocrinology, and GI without triggering a unified oncological protocol.
*   **System failure:** Incidental pancreatic cysts (IPMNs) found on routine CT/MRI scans for unrelated issues are routinely lost to follow-up due to poor longitudinal tracking in hospital EMRs.

3) Detection window & gap (concise)
*   **Earliest detectable signal (research / ideal):** 18–36 months before clinical symptoms (via exosomal markers, hypermethylated ctDNA, or algorithmic EMR detection of new-onset diabetes + weight loss).
*   **Typical clinical detection:** Stage III/IV (locally advanced or metastatic), typically triggered by obstructive jaundice or severe, intractable pain.
*   **Gap to close:** 12 to 24 months. Shifting diagnosis from Stage III/IV to Stage I/II increases the 5-year survival rate from an abysmal 3% to over 40%.

4) What’s being used today (gold standard + emergent)
*   **Gold standard(s):** Multiphase pancreatic protocol CT, Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS) with Fine-Needle Aspiration (FNA), and serum CA19-9.
*   **Emerging research / tools:** Multi-cancer early detection (MCED) blood tests (e.g., Galleri targeting methylation patterns), exosomal protein markers (e.g., GPC1, EphA2), and AI-driven EMR risk stratification models (e.g., ENDPAC score for new-onset diabetes).
*   **Main limitations:** MCED tests still struggle with Stage I sensitivity due to low ctDNA shedding; EUS is too invasive, operator-dependent, and expensive for population screening; AI models suffer from alert fatigue and lack workflow integration.

5) Where healthcare is failing (operational insight)
*   **Screening point that drops the ball:** Incidental findings. Radiology reports noting "pancreatic cysts" lack automated reflex pathways, relying on primary care physicians to manually schedule and track 6-to-12-month surveillance imaging.
*   **Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:** Automating the identification and reflex referral of patients over 50 presenting with new-onset atypical diabetes and concurrent weight loss.
*   **High-risk population missed:** Patients with familial risk (BRCA1/2, PALB2, CDKN2A mutations) who never receive genetic counseling, and older adults whose sudden-onset diabetes is misclassified as standard Type 2.

6) 3 High-leverage solution ideas (practical, ranked)
*   **[Idea A — quick pilotable] EMR "NOD-Weight" Alert Pilot:** Run a 90-day pilot deploying a lightweight algorithm on primary care EMRs flagging patients >50yo with New-Onset Diabetes (NOD) + >5% body weight loss. Trigger an automated reflex order for CA19-9 and a fast-track MRI/EUS referral. *Metrics to collect:* Flag rate, referral completion percentage, diagnostic yield (cysts or early masses found).
*   **[Idea B — scalable tech / workflow change] Automated Incidental Cyst Tracker:** Deploy an NLP-based tool that scans radiology reports for "pancreatic cyst" or "IPMN." Automatically add these patients to a longitudinal surveillance registry with automated SMS scheduling nudges for both patient and provider. *Resource checklist:* EMR integration (FHIR/HL7), NLP module, patient-comms API. *Expected impact:* Reduce loss-to-follow-up for high-risk cysts from ~60% to <15%.
*   **[Idea C — research / product] Salivary/Capillary Exosome Point-of-Care:** Develop a microfluidic assay targeting PDAC-specific exosomes (e.g., GPC1) that bypass the desmoplastic stroma better than cell-free DNA. *Highest upside:* Can be deployed in routine dental or primary care visits. *Tests needed:* Validation of exosome stability in saliva vs. plasma. *Collaborators to approach:* High-risk familial pancreatic cancer surveillance clinics (e.g., Johns Hopkins or MD Anderson).
