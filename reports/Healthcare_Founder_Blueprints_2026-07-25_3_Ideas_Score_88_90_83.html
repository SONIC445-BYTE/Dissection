1) TriageOS: Autonomous Waitlist Load Balancing — Score: 88/100

ID: c8a2b5e1-7d3f-4f9a-9b1c-8e2d4a6f5b3c

TL;DR: An AI-agentic layer that dynamically audits hospital waitlists to route low-acuity patients to asynchronous care, unlocking capacity for a $150B global bottleneck.

Evidence

  • Tens of thousands of UK NHS patients wait over two years for routine treatments due to systemic capacity failures. — [source]
  • 62.3% of Brazilians gave up on primary care in 2025, with 46.9% citing overcrowding and wait times. — [source]
  • US wait times have stagnated, averaging 41 days nationally and up to 57 days in major hubs like Boston. — [source]

Problem

Globally, patient demand has vastly outpaced physical infrastructure and physician availability. In the US, patients wait up to 57 days for appointments, while in the UK, tens of thousands wait over two years. This structural bottleneck forces millions, like the 62% of Brazilians who abandoned care, to forgo treatment entirely until conditions become acute and exponentially more expensive to treat.

Why It Will Remain Unsolved

  • Physical infrastructure and human capital (physicians) require decade-long lead times and massive capital expenditure to scale.
  • Legacy EMRs (Epic, Cerner) operate as static databases rather than dynamic, predictive queueing systems.
  • Misaligned incentives in fee-for-service models prioritize high-acuity surgical cases over efficient primary care throughput.

AI-First Solution

TriageOS sits on top of legacy EMRs, utilizing specialized LLMs to continuously ingest referral notes, historical patient data, and current queue lengths. It autonomously scores patient acuity and identifies individuals who can be safely diverted to telehealth, automated monitoring, or lower-tier providers, dynamically rebalancing the waitlist.

  • Core: Predictive clinical NLP for dynamic acuity scoring and waitlist diversion.
  • Acuity-based auto-rebooking: Automatically texts low-risk patients with options to switch to virtual care within 48 hours.
  • Bi-directional FHIR integration: Writes updated appointment statuses and triage notes directly back into Epic/Cerner.
  • Outcome feedback loop: Tracks 30-day readmission or ER visit rates for diverted patients to continuously calibrate the safety threshold of the diversion model.

Key Metrics

  • North Star: Average waitlist reduction (in days) per deployed department
  • Traction KPI: Number of hospital departments/clinics deployed
  • Economic KPI: Gross margin on diversion fees (Target: 85%+)

  • Core Stack: vLLM for inference, Qdrant for vector search of clinical guidelines, Temporal for workflow orchestration.
  • Datasets: De-identified EMR referral logs, historical appointment wait times, clinical triage guidelines.
  • Integrations: Epic FHIR API, Cerner Ignite APIs, Twilio for SMS outreach.

Regulatory Path

  • US FDA: Class I Clinical Decision Support (exempt from 510(k) if the physician makes the final call).
  • Clinical validation via retrospective shadow studies proving non-inferiority to human triage nurses.
  • HIPAA/GDPR compliance with local data residency (e.g., AWS London for NHS data).

2) FiscalCare: LLM-Driven Claims Auditing & Deficit Prevention — Score: 90/100

ID: f9d3a2b1-8c4e-4f5a-9d2b-1e3c4a5b6d7e

TL;DR: An autonomous medical coding and claims validation engine designed to stop multi-million dollar monthly deficits in universal and private health systems.

Evidence

  • Indonesia's state insurer BPJS is running a Rp 2 trillion ($111.5M USD) monthly deficit, threatening collapse by 2027. — [source]
  • US medical debt has reached $220 billion, trapping roughly 100 million Americans in financial toxicity. — [source]
  • Mexico's IMSS-Bienestar budget dropped 24.9% to just $218 USD per capita, pushing costs onto the poorest citizens. — [source]

Problem

Universal healthcare systems in emerging markets (like Indonesia's BPJS) are buckling under massive, unsustainable deficits—losing up to $111M USD monthly due to inefficient claims auditing and fraud. Simultaneously, in privatized systems like the US, opaque billing and claims denials result in $220 billion of medical debt, financially ruining patients. The administrative bloat of processing, denying, and appealing claims destroys value across all system types.

Why It Will Remain Unsolved

  • In emerging markets, state insurers lack the technical infrastructure and engineering talent to audit millions of claims automatically, relying on slow manual reviews.
  • In the US, profit-driven intermediaries (insurers, PBMs) intentionally utilize complex, opaque rules to delay payments and extract value.
  • Medical coding requires highly specialized, expensive human labor that cannot scale to meet the volume of global healthcare transactions.

AI-First Solution

FiscalCare deploys a Graph-RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) architecture to autonomously audit hospital claims before submission. By cross-referencing raw clinical physician notes with complex state payer guidelines (like BPJS or CMS rules) in real-time, the AI agent perfectly codes the claim, flags missing clinical justification, and predicts the likelihood of denial, ensuring state funds are allocated correctly and hospitals get paid instantly.

  • Core: Graph-RAG based autonomous medical coding and denial prediction.
  • Pre-submission denial prediction: Flags claims with a >10% chance of denial and highlights the exact missing clinical documentation needed.
  • Payer Policy API Integration: Continuously ingests and updates a vector database with the latest state/private payer billing guidelines.
  • Continuous Adjudication Learning: Ingests 835 remittance files to learn from actual payer decisions, constantly fine-tuning the denial prediction model.

Key Metrics

  • North Star: Percentage reduction in initial claim denial rates
  • Traction KPI: Number of covered lives / claim volume processed
  • Economic KPI: Net Revenue Retention (NRR) and % of recovered revenue

12-Week MVP

WeeksDeliverableSuccess Metric
W1-W4Ingest 100,000 historical claims (837s) and remittances (835s) to build the baseline denial prediction model.Achieve 0.85 AUC in predicting historical claim denials.
W5-W8Build the Graph-RAG engine that maps raw clinical text to specific ICD-10/CPT codes and payer rules.Achieve >90% accuracy against a gold-standard dataset of human-coded claims.
W9-W12Deploy a live dashboard for hospital billing staff to review AI-flagged claims prior to clearinghouse submission.Reduce the denial rate of the pilot hospital's submitted cohort by 15%.

Tech & Data Needs

  • Core Stack: PyTorch, Neo4j (Graph Database), Llama-3 (fine-tuned for medical billing), LangChain.
  • Datasets: 837 (Claims) and 835 (Remittance) EDI files, clinical encounter notes, payer policy manuals.
  • Integrations: Clearinghouse APIs (e.g., Change Healthcare, Availity), Hospital RCM software (Epic Resolute).

Regulatory Path

  • Non-clinical administrative software: Exempt from FDA/CDSCO medical device classification.
  • Strict adherence to data privacy laws (HIPAA in US, PDPA in Indonesia) requiring robust PHI de-identification.
  • SOC2 Type II and ISO 27001 certifications required for enterprise payer/hospital procurement.

3) ProcureMed AI: Predictive Supply Chain Resiliency — Score: 83/100

ID: d4e5f6a7-8b9c-4d0e-1f2a-3b4c5d6e7f8a

TL;DR: A federated forecasting platform that predicts localized pharmaceutical and equipment shortages 90 days in advance, preventing fatal care disruptions.

Evidence

  • Medicine shortages in Mexico's IMSS system spiked 43% in 2025, alongside a $1.06 billion reduction in supplies. — [source]
  • 34.4% of Brazilian patients cite a direct lack of medical equipment at facilities as a primary barrier to care. — [source]
  • Nigeria's primary healthcare system is failing millions due to poor physical infrastructure and lack of essential resources. — [source]

Problem

Chronic supply chain fragility and bureaucratic budget cuts are causing severe shortages of essential medicines and basic equipment across emerging markets. In Mexico, a 43% spike in medicine shortages and massive budget cuts have left chronic disease patients without life-saving drugs. In Brazil, over a third of patients face facilities simply lacking the physical equipment to treat them. This transforms manageable conditions into fatal outcomes purely due to logistical failures.

Why It Will Remain Unsolved

  • Global pharmaceutical supply chains are heavily centralized in China and India; local markets cannot easily spin up domestic manufacturing.
  • When local currencies devalue, imported drugs become instantly unaffordable for state health budgets, causing abrupt stockouts.
  • Legacy hospital ERP systems rely on historical, static demand rather than predictive, epidemiological, and macroeconomic forecasting.

AI-First Solution

ProcureMed AI utilizes Temporal Fusion Transformers to analyze disparate data streams—hospital inventory levels, regional EMR morbidity trends, global API supply chain alerts, and real-time currency FX rates. It predicts specific drug and equipment stockouts up to 90 days in advance at the facility level, autonomously generating procurement alerts and suggesting alternative generic sourcing to Ministry of Health officials before the shortage hits the frontline.

  • Core: Multivariate time-series forecasting for localized pharmaceutical demand and supply shocks.
  • 90-Day Stockout Alerting: Dashboard highlighting specific SKUs at risk of depletion with probability scores.
  • Automated Alternative Sourcing: Recommends equivalent generic APIs or alternative regional distributors when primary suppliers face disruptions.
  • ERP Integration: Pushes predictive purchase orders directly into SAP or Oracle Health for one-click approval by procurement officers.

Key Metrics

  • North Star: Number of stockout days avoided across deployed facilities
  • Traction KPI: Total procurement spend managed through the platform
  • Economic KPI: Percentage of procurement savings generated via early/alternative sourcing
