## Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — MASLD/MASH (Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease / Steatohepatitis) — 2026-07-16

---

### 1) Snapshot (one line)

**MASLD/MASH** — why early diagnosis fails in practice: A clinically silent disease affecting **~38% of the global adult population** goes undetected until advanced fibrosis or cirrhosis because the only gold standard is an invasive biopsy, first-line blood tests (FIB-4) fail in the highest-risk populations, and no systematic screening protocol exists in primary care despite clear guidelines.

---

### 2) Why Early Diagnosis Fails (3–5 bullets)

- **Biological barrier:** MASLD is asymptomatic through Stages F0–F2 (simple steatosis → early fibrosis). Liver enzymes (ALT/AST) are frequently *normal* in early MASLD — the disease hides behind a clean blood panel. Fat must exceed 20–33% of hepatocytes before standard ultrasound can even detect steatosis. Fibrosis progresses at only ~0.29 stages/year, meaning the window for early intervention spans *decades* yet is almost never utilized.

- **Test limitation:** Liver biopsy (gold standard) is invasive, captures 1/50,000th of the liver, has significant inter-observer variability, and is completely unscalable for a disease affecting 1 in 3 adults. FIB-4 — the recommended first-line NIT — has documented failure in patients with T2D and obesity (the exact high-risk cohort), producing false reassurance. POCUS misses steatosis below 20–33% threshold and performs poorly in high-BMI patients.

- **System failure (primary care):** The 2024 EASL-EASD-EASO guidelines mandate FIB-4 screening in all T2D and obese patients — but real-world implementation is negligible. Endocrinologists manage glucose; hepatologists manage cirrhosis; nobody owns the F0–F2 diagnostic window in primary care. EMR-based identification using ICD codes misses **>40% of actual MASLD cases** because early-stage patients never receive a formal liver diagnosis code.

- **System failure (referral pathway):** Even when FIB-4 is ordered, there is no standardized reflex testing protocol. A borderline FIB-4 (1.3–2.67) — the "grey zone" affecting millions — rarely triggers automated ELF or FibroScan follow-up. Patients sit in limbo for months or years.

- **Awareness gap:** ~2 in 3 patients are only formally diagnosed at or within 6 months of a severe liver complication (decompensation, hepatocellular carcinoma, or transplant evaluation). The disease is found late *by accident* — on abdominal imaging ordered for something else.

### 4) What's Being Used Today (Gold Standard + Emergent)

**Gold Standard(s):**
- **Liver biopsy** (histopathology with NAS score + fibrosis staging) — still required for definitive MASH diagnosis in clinical trials; increasingly replaced by NITs in routine care post-resmetirom approval
- **Transient elastography (FibroScan)** — widely used for liver stiffness; combined with CAP for steatosis grading

**Emerging Research / Tools:**
| Tool | Type | What It Detects | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NIS4** (miR-34a-5p, A2M, HbA1c, YKL-40) | Blood panel | At-risk MASH | Validated; used in trials |
| **ELF Score** (HA, PIIINP, TIMP-1) | Blood panel | Fibrosis F2+ | CE-marked; FDA-cleared |
| **PRO-C3** | Blood biomarker | Active fibrogenesis | Phase 3 trial use |
| **FAST Score** (FibroScan-AST) | Composite | Fibrotic MASH | Validated, guideline-emerging |
| **MAST Score** (MRI + AST + platelets) | Composite | At-risk MASH | High accuracy, MRI-dependent |
| **NASHMap** | AI/ML (14 EMR parameters) | MASLD/MASH prediction | Research; EMR-deployable |
| **Eicosanoid plasma signature** | Lipidomics panel | Early MASLD | Research stage (JLR 2024) |
| **AI-POCUS (deep learning)** | Imaging + AI | Hepatic steatosis grading | Emerging; handheld devices |
| **FNI Index** | Blood calculation | Long-term MASLD mortality in T2D | Outperforms FIB-4 in T2D cohorts |

**Main Limitations:**
- NIS4 and PRO-C3: proprietary, expensive, not widely reimbursed outside trials
- ELF: requires specialized analyzer (Siemens ADVIA); not available in most community labs
- MRI-PDFF (most accurate for steatosis quantification): expensive, limited access, not scalable
- AI-POCUS: operator-dependent; handheld quantitative ultrasound still in validation
- All NITs: performance degrades in extreme BMI, T2D, and elderly patients — the exact target population

### 5) Where Healthcare Is Failing (Operational Insight)

**Screening point that drops the ball:**
Primary care and endocrinology clinics — the *first and best* opportunity to screen high-risk patients (T2D, obesity, metabolic syndrome) — have no embedded liver-screening reflex. A diabetic patient on metformin for 10 years may never receive a FIB-4 calculation despite it being derivable from routine CBC + metabolic panel already in the chart.

**Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:**
**Automated FIB-4 calculation + reflex ELF ordering** embedded in EMR for all patients with T2D or BMI >30 who have routine labs. This requires zero new tests — FIB-4 uses age, AST, ALT, platelets — all already collected. A simple EMR alert rule + standing reflex order for ELF in the grey zone (FIB-4 1.3–2.67) could identify thousands of at-risk patients per hospital system within a quarter.

**High-risk population missed:**
1. **T2D patients in endocrinology/primary care** — managed for glucose, never screened for liver fibrosis despite 50%+ MASLD prevalence in this group
2. **Patients with "normal" ALT** — up to 79% of MASLD patients have ALT within normal range; clinicians falsely reassured
3. **Young-onset metabolic patients (age <45)** — FIB-4 is mathematically age-dependent; it systematically *under-scores* younger patients, creating false negatives in a growing cohort of young obese/diabetic individuals
4. **Ethnic minorities (South Asian, Hispanic)** — higher MASLD prevalence at lower BMI thresholds; standard BMI cutoffs miss them

### 7) First-Principles Signal Hunt (What We Should Measure Earlier)

**Hidden signal candidate:**
The liver's earliest response to metabolic stress is *lipid dysregulation and inflammatory signaling* — not fibrosis. Three underexplored early signal sources:

1. **Plasma eicosanoid signature** (arachidonic acid metabolites — prostaglandins, leukotrienes, HETEs): A 2024 JLR study identified a specific eicosanoid panel with high diagnostic accuracy for early MASLD — *before* fibrosis begins. These are measurable in standard plasma with targeted lipidomics.

2. **Circulating miRNA panel** (miR-34a-5p, miR-122, miR-192): Liver-specific miRNAs are released into blood during hepatocyte stress and steatosis, *preceding* fibrosis. miR-34a-5p is already incorporated in NIS4. A low-cost RT-PCR panel could be deployable in routine labs.

3. **Gut microbiome-derived metabolites** (trimethylamine N-oxide [TMAO], short-chain fatty acids): TMAO elevation precedes MASLD progression and is measurable in urine — a non-invasive, scalable signal currently ignored in clinical workflows.

**Minimal sampling change needed:**
- Standard EDTA plasma tube (already collected in routine labs) — sufficient for eicosanoid lipidomics and miRNA panels
- Morning spot urine — sufficient for TMAO
- *No new phlebotomy visit required* if piggybacked on routine T2D monitoring labs

**Recurring diagnostic failure pattern emerging across briefs:**

> **The "Silent Progression + Siloed Care + Missing Reflex" Triad**

MASLD reinforces a pattern now visible across multiple high-burden diseases:
1. **Silent biological progression** — the disease causes measurable molecular changes years before symptoms or organ damage (cf. preclinical Alzheimer's, early endometriosis, subclinical PAH)
2. **Siloed specialty care** — the organ system at risk is managed by a different specialist than the one seeing the patient most frequently (hepatology vs. endocrinology; neurology vs. primary care)
3. **Missing reflex protocol** — even when a first-line test exists and is ordered, there is no automated cascade to the confirmatory test; the result sits in the chart without triggering action

**What's generalizable:**
The highest-ROI intervention across *all* these diseases is **not a new biomarker or device** — it's an **EMR-embedded reflex cascade** that converts existing, already-ordered routine labs into a screening signal. FIB-4 from routine CBC + metabolic panel. p-tau217 from routine memory clinic blood draw. FNE score from routine ECG. The data is already being collected. The diagnostic gap is an **information routing problem**, not a biology problem.

A **universal "silent disease" EMR risk-scoring layer** — a single AI/ML platform that runs population-level risk models for 5–10 silently-progressing diseases simultaneously (MASLD, preclinical Alzheimer's, PAH, ALS prodrome, early ovarian cancer) using *only routine labs already in the chart* — could be the highest-leverage diagnostic infrastructure play of the decade.
