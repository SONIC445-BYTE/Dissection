1) FlowOps AI: Predictive Discharge & Step-Down Orchestrator — Score: 84/100

ID: e4b3b8c2-1f4a-4b9e-8e3d-7d2f9a1c5b6e

TL;DR: Predicts patient discharge bottlenecks at admission and automatically secures community care beds, eliminating the UK/Singapore 12-hour emergency corridor wait and tapping into a $25B market.

Evidence

  • In May 2026, 50,000 patients in the UK waited over 12 hours for an emergency admission, 121 times higher than pre-pandemic levels. — [source]
  • NHS England recorded an average of 2,241 daily instances of 'corridor care' in emergency departments alone in May 2026. — [source]
  • The UK waiting list reached 7.22 million cases, with median wait times of 11.9 weeks, driven by systemic capacity failures. — [source]

Problem

Hospitals in the UK and Singapore are suffering from a catastrophic failure in patient flow. Emergency departments are forced to treat patients in non-clinical spaces ('corridor care') because acute beds are occupied by patients who are medically fit for discharge but cannot leave. This bottleneck is caused by an inability to quickly match and fund step-down social care or rehabilitation beds, creating a backlog that resulted in 50,000 UK patients waiting over 12 hours for admission in a single month.

Why It Will Remain Unsolved

  • Structural constraint: Acute hospital care and community social care operate on entirely disconnected funding and operational models.
  • Data fragmentation: Hospitals lack real-time visibility into the availability of beds in private or community-run care homes.
  • Adoption friction: Discharge planning is highly manual, relying on phone calls, whiteboards, and fragmented local authority assessments.

AI-First Solution

FlowOps AI uses federated machine learning across hospital EMRs and local authority social care databases to predict a patient's exact discharge needs and length of stay on day one of admission. It then acts as an automated broker, securing step-down beds before the patient is even medically cleared.

  • Core: Federated learning across acute EMRs and fragmented social care capacity systems to predict discharge delays.
  • Length of Stay (LoS) & Discharge Pathway Predictor: Reduces delayed transfers of care (DTOC) days by 30%.
  • Automated Social Care Brokerage: Integrates via API with local care home management systems to auto-secure beds.
  • Readmission Risk Feedback Loop: Continuously adjusts placement recommendations based on 30-day patient readmission rates.

Key Metrics

  • North Star: Reduction in delayed transfer of care (DTOC) bed days
  • Traction KPI: Number of NHS Trusts / Regional Health Systems signed
  • Economic KPI: SaaS ARR + transactional fee per successful step-down placement

2) Aegis Health: Algorithmic Prior-Auth & Appeal Shield — Score: 92/100

ID: a1b2c3d4-e5f6-4a5b-8c7d-9e0f1a2b3c4d

TL;DR: An AI clearinghouse that fights payer denial algorithms with clinically un-deniable, auto-generated prior authorization and appeal packets, unlocking a $150B RCM market.

Evidence

  • 1 in 5 U.S. adults with private insurance were denied coverage for doctor-recommended care in the past year, causing 41% to delay care. — [source]
  • Nearly 70% of patients experiencing claims denials faced higher out-of-pocket costs, and 43% were forced into medical debt. — [source]
  • Insurers use proprietary AI algorithms shielded as 'trade secrets' to deny claims, preventing doctors from knowing the denial basis. — [source]

Problem

In the US and UAE, private insurers have weaponized AI to automatically deny legitimate medical claims and prior authorizations. This algorithmic trap relies on the fact that only a fraction of patients and providers have the resources to navigate the complex appeals process. Consequently, 1 in 5 patients are denied care, 41% face delays, and 43% are pushed into medical debt, while insurers boost their margins by shielding their criteria as 'trade secrets'.

Why It Will Remain Unsolved

  • Misaligned incentives: Insurer profitability is directly tied to minimizing the medical loss ratio (MLR) by denying care.
  • Structural constraint: The legal shielding of payer algorithms as 'trade secrets' prevents providers from proactively meeting criteria.
  • Economics barrier: Providers cannot afford the administrative bloat required to manually fight algorithmic denials at scale.

AI-First Solution

Aegis Health provides providers with an aggressive counter-AI. It ingests patient EMR data and maps it against a massive, continuously updated vector database of payer policies and historical denial patterns. It then auto-generates mathematically and clinically un-deniable prior authorization requests and appeals, essentially reverse-engineering the payer's 'trade secret' algorithms.

  • Core: RAG-based LLM architecture that cross-references clinical EMR data with a dynamic vector database of payer medical policies.
  • Auto-Appeal Generator: Increases first-level appeal win rate by 40%.
  • Pre-Claim AI Auditor: Integrates via SMART on FHIR to flag missing clinical documentation before submission.
  • Payer Algorithm Reverse-Engineering: Feedback loop that updates the policy vector database every time an insurer issues a denial reason code.

Key Metrics

  • North Star: First-pass prior authorization approval percentage
  • Traction KPI: Number of specialty clinics / health systems live
  • Economic KPI: Gross margin on contingency revenue from overturned denials

3) SUS-Bypass: WhatsApp-Native Triage & Referral Engine — Score: 85/100

ID: f5e4d3c2-b1a0-9f8e-7d6c-5b4a3f2e1d0c

TL;DR: A conversational AI that triages patients abandoning public health systems and auto-generates bureaucratic referral paperwork, capturing a $20B universal care market.

Evidence

  • 62.3% of Brazilians who needed medical care in the Primary Health Care system in the last year did not seek it, primarily due to overcrowding. — [source]
  • Of the patients who attempted to seek care, 40.5% were unable to receive it due to long wait times and lack of professionals. — [source]
  • 51.5% of Brazilian patients complained about the severe bureaucracy involved in getting referrals to specialists. — [source]

Problem

In countries with underfunded universal healthcare (like Brazil and Mexico), millions of citizens are effectively locked out of the system. Over 62% of Brazilians actively avoid the public health system when sick due to systemic friction, overcrowding, and severe referral bureaucracy. This forces patients to abandon care, leading to easily preventable chronic conditions escalating into expensive acute emergencies.

Why It Will Remain Unsolved

  • Structural constraint: Promising free, unlimited healthcare without sufficient tax revenue forces systems to use 'waiting lists' and 'bureaucracy' to ration care.
  • Adoption friction: Public health portals are poorly designed, requiring patients to physically visit overcrowded clinics just to get a paper referral.
  • Economics barrier: Governments lack the capital to build more physical clinics or hire enough doctors to meet demand.

AI-First Solution

SUS-Bypass meets patients where they already are: WhatsApp. It uses a fine-tuned LLM to conduct clinical triage in local languages, scores urgency, and automatically generates the exact, compliant bureaucratic referral forms required by the public health system (e.g., SUS in Brazil). It then routes non-urgent cases to low-cost private micro-clinics or telehealth, offloading public system pressure.

  • Core: Conversational AI triage deployed via WhatsApp Business API, combined with automated government form generation.
  • Clinical Urgency Scoring: Accurately categorizes patient acuity to reduce unnecessary ER visits by 40%.
  • Auto-Referral Generation: Fills out and digitally signs public health specialist referral forms (e.g., SISREG).
  • Micro-Clinic Routing: Connects triaged patients to available low-cost private providers if public wait times exceed safe limits.

Key Metrics

  • North Star: Number of successful triages completed via WhatsApp
  • Traction KPI: Municipal government contracts signed / Telco bundles activated
  • Economic KPI: Cost per triage vs. traditional primary care visit cost
