Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — Endometriosis — 2026-05-17

1) Snapshot (one line)
Endometriosis — why early diagnosis fails in practice: Reliance on invasive laparoscopy and operator-dependent imaging leaves a 7–10 year diagnostic void for a disease driven by localized tissue misplacement.

2) Why early diagnosis fails (3–5 bullets)
*   **Biological barrier:** Superficial peritoneal lesions do not produce massive systemic immune spikes, and symptoms overlap heavily with "normalized" dysmenorrhea or IBS.
*   **Test limitation:** Routine transvaginal ultrasound (TVUS) easily misses superficial disease; definitive diagnosis still largely requires surgical laparoscopy (high cost, invasive, requires general anesthesia).
*   **System failure:** Severe clinical gaslighting and systemic stigma normalize debilitating pain, while generalist sonographers lack the specialized training to perform dynamic mapping (e.g., the "sliding sign") for deep infiltrating endometriosis (DIE).

3) Detection window & gap (concise)
*   **Earliest detectable signal (research / ideal):** Epigenetic/miRNA shifts in saliva or menstrual effluent (detectable months to years before severe architectural distortion of the pelvis).
*   **Typical clinical detection:** 7 to 10 years post-symptom onset (usually discovered via surgery after an infertility crisis or chronic pain emergency).
*   **Gap to close:** 7+ years. Closing this window shifts treatment from radical excisional surgery and fertility rescue to early medical suppression and preserved quality of life.

4) What’s being used today (gold standard + emergent)
*   **Gold standard(s):** Diagnostic laparoscopy with histological confirmation; Advanced TVUS/MRI for deep disease mapping.
*   **Emerging research / tools:** Salivary miRNA panels (e.g., Ziwig Endotest, leveraging 109 miRNAs + AI, ~95% accuracy); menstrual effluent liquid biopsies (NextGen Jane, The ROSE Study).
*   **Main limitations:** Laparoscopy is invasive. Saliva NGS panels have a 1–3 week turnaround time and lack widespread reimbursement/infrastructure. Menstrual effluent faces severe transit-degradation and standardization challenges.

5) Where healthcare is failing (operational insight)
*   **Screening point that drops the ball:** Primary care and general OB/GYN visits where generic pelvic ultrasounds are ordered instead of specialized endometriosis mapping protocols.
*   **Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:** Unstructured ultrasound reporting. Generalists do not document the "sliding sign" or specific pelvic compartments (like the pouch of Douglas), leading to dead-end diagnoses and missed surgical triage.
*   **High-risk population missed:** Adolescents presenting with severe dysmenorrhea who are empirically put on birth control without diagnostic workup, masking the disease progression until they attempt pregnancy in their late 20s or 30s.

*   **[Idea B — scalable tech / workflow change] At-Home Menstrual Effluent Stabilization Kit:** Partner with a centralized lab to pilot a proprietary buffer-solution tampon/cup for at-home collection. *Resource checklist:* FDA-compliant collection tubes, RNA stabilization buffer, logistics partner. *Expected impact:* Bypasses clinical gaslighting entirely by allowing direct-to-consumer molecular screening for earlier intervention.
*   **[Idea C — research / product] Saliva miRNA Point-of-Care Proxy:** NGS is currently too slow and expensive for broad screening. *Highest upside:* Develop an isothermal amplification (LAMP) or electrochemical sensor for the top 3–5 most predictive miRNAs from the 109-plex salivary panel to bring turnaround time from 3 weeks to 30 minutes. *Collaborators:* Ziwig, university biosensor labs.
