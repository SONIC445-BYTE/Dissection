# JARVIS Evidence Foundation — Global Knowledge Graph and Normalized Problem Database

**Purpose:** This is the analytical input to the product decision document. It was built before strategic selection. It normalizes overlapping evidence from the 283-file repository into 38 problem nodes.

**Counting method.** “Frequency” is the number of repository documents in which a pre-specified keyword set for the normalized problem occurred. It is a *document-presence signal*, not a count of independent incidents or a measure of market size. Repeated daily-report templates can inflate it. Sources are abbreviated as **MI** (132 India market-intelligence reports), **FB** (115 Founder Blueprints), **EI** (26 ELITE workforce/system reports), and **O** (10 other files). Countries named in the corpus are India most often, with selected FB examples in the US, GCC/UAE/Saudi Arabia, Singapore, and the Global South.

## C. Problem-node adjacency (causal links used in synthesis)

- **Documentation/records (10) + unstructured data (23) + interoperability (5)** → coding defects (17), claims denial (1), cash-flow leakage (11), medico-legal risk (20), and broken handoffs (22).
- **Workforce shortage (2) + burnout (3) + pay exploitation (38) + violence (13)** → wait/queues (12), triage failure (4), quality risk (19), and further attrition.
- **ED crowding (4) + bed capacity (33) + discharge gap (34) + housekeeping (37)** → boarding, family conflict/trust loss (8), staff violence exposure (13), and delayed care.
- **Procurement/stock-outs (14) + pharmacy (9) + equipment readiness (31) + ambulance readiness (27)** → treatment delay and emergency risk.
- **Government-scheme friction (16) + prior auth (24) + claims denial (1) + cash-flow (11)** → hospital scheme withdrawal, access loss, and staffing pressure.
- **Trust/grievance (8) + weak consent/audit (28) + medico-legal evidence (20)** → conflict, reputational crisis, and defensive medicine.
- **Rural/offline access (6) + referral gaps (30) + diagnostic delays (19) + credential gaps (25)** → late presentation and avoidable tertiary-care overload.

## D. Evidence limits that constrain interpretation

1. Frequencies measure source-document presence; they cannot establish prevalence, causal effect, willingness to pay, or clinical efficacy.
2. The corpus is India-heavy and contains repeated template reports; market intelligence and Founder Blueprint material should not be mistaken for independent validation.
3. Proposed ventures were excluded from the later real-company competitor map.
4. “Financial impact,” time, and human impact are classified qualitatively except where a source document supplied a statistic; no cross-report aggregation of those figures was attempted.
5. The database is a product-discovery normalization, not clinical guidance, actuarial analysis, or a regulatory determination.
