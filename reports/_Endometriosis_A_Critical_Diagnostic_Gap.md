1) Snapshot (one line)
Endometriosis — why early diagnosis fails in practice: Systemic normalization of female pelvic pain combined with a reliance on invasive surgery as the sole definitive diagnostic standard.

2) Why early diagnosis fails (3–5 bullets)
*   **Biological barrier:** Highly heterogeneous lesions that do not reliably correlate with symptom severity; lack of a single, highly expressed systemic protein biomarker.
*   **Test limitation:** Standard pelvic ultrasounds miss superficial peritoneal lesions completely; the gold-standard laparoscopy is invasive, expensive, and requires general anesthesia.
*   **System failure:** Medical gaslighting (normalization of dysmenorrhea); frequent misdiagnosis as Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS); massive surgical waitlists for diagnostic laparoscopy.

3) Detection window & gap (concise)
*   **Earliest detectable signal (research / ideal):** Altered microRNA profiles in serum/saliva and stromal cells in menstrual effluent (detectable years before severe adhesion formation).
*   **Typical clinical detection:** 7 to 10 years post-symptom onset, usually after severe chronic pain, organ tethering, or infertility presents.
*   **Gap to close:** 7–10 years. Closing this prevents irreversible pelvic scarring, centralized nervous system pain, and infertility.

4) What’s being used today (gold standard + emergent)
*   **Gold standard(s):** Diagnostic laparoscopy with histological biopsy confirmation.
*   **Emerging research / tools:** Menstrual effluent testing (e.g., Endometrics), serum/saliva microRNA panels (Yale research), AI-enhanced transvaginal ultrasound (TVS) for deep infiltrating endometriosis.
*   **Main limitations:** Surgery carries risks and high costs; emerging biomarkers are not yet FDA-cleared and lack standardized clinical cutoffs; advanced TVS is highly operator-dependent and not widely accessible.

5) Where healthcare is failing (operational insight)
*   **Screening point that drops the ball:** Primary care and adolescent gynecology. Patients presenting with severe cyclic pain are empirically given birth control without a diagnostic workup, masking the disease while it silently progresses.
*   **Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:** Misdiagnosis routing. Many patients are routed to gastroenterology for "IBS" without a standardized, symptom-based gynecological rule-out.
*   **High-risk population missed:** Adolescents with severe dysmenorrhea and women presenting with unexplained infertility.
