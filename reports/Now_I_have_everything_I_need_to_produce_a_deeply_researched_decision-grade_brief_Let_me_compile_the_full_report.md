## Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — MASLD/MASH (Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease / Steatohepatitis) — 2026-06-21

---

### 1) Snapshot (one line)

**MASLD/MASH** — why early diagnosis fails in practice: A silent, decade-long fibrotic progression affecting **1.3 billion people globally** goes undetected because the only approved first-line screen (FIB-4) has ~50% sensitivity for the clinically actionable F2–F3 fibrosis stage, primary care lacks the workflow to act on indeterminate results, and the disease produces no symptoms until cirrhosis or liver cancer appears.

---

### 2) Why Early Diagnosis Fails (3–5 bullets)

- **Biological barrier:** MASLD is asymptomatic for 10–30 years; liver fibrosis stages F0–F3 produce no pain, jaundice, or organ dysfunction. Patients feel well until decompensated cirrhosis or HCC arrives — by which point the therapeutic window for disease-modifying therapy (e.g., resmetirom) has closed.

- **Test limitation — FIB-4 is a coin-toss for F2/F3:** The FIB-4 index (age × AST / platelets × √ALT) is the guideline-recommended first-line screen, yet its sensitivity for detecting ≥F2 fibrosis is only **~45–55%** at the standard cutoff of 1.3. Critically, both obesity and T2DM — the two most prevalent comorbidities in MASLD — **independently reduce FIB-4 accuracy**, making it least reliable in precisely the patients who need it most.

- **Test limitation — sequential NIT pathway breaks down at the indeterminate zone:** FibroScan (VCTE) offers 75–85% sensitivity for F2/F3 but **fails or overestimates fibrosis in Class 3 obesity** (BMI >40), the fastest-growing MASLD subgroup. ELF scores are more robust but require specialized analyzers unavailable in most primary care labs. Together, 62–73% of patients with biopsy-confirmed MASH F2–F3 are **missed by current NIT combinations**.

- **System failure — primary care triage and referral collapse:** Only **16% of T2DM patients** with MASLD have a documented diagnosis in their EHR. FIB-4 is rarely calculated automatically. When it is, "indeterminate" results (1.3–2.67) — which represent the majority of MASH patients — trigger no standardized reflex action: no automatic FibroScan order, no hepatology referral, no follow-up timeline. The pathway simply stops.

- **System failure — resmetirom creates a diagnostic-therapeutic paradox:** The 2024 FDA approval of resmetirom (Rezdiffra) — the first-ever drug for MASH F2/F3 — created an urgent need to identify eligible patients. But the drug's clinical trial criteria required **biopsy confirmation**, and real-world NITs cannot reliably replicate that precision. Prescribers either over-prescribe (based on weak NIT data) or under-prescribe (awaiting biopsy that never gets ordered). Pharma has a drug; medicine lacks the diagnostic infrastructure to find the patients.

### 4) What's Being Used Today (Gold Standard + Emergent)

**Gold Standards:**
- **Liver biopsy** — histological staging (Metavir/NASH-CRN score); sensitivity near 100% but invasive, costly ($1,500–$3,500), sampling error risk (~25%), and impossible to scale to 1.3 billion at-risk patients
- **FIB-4 index** — guideline-recommended first-line NIT (AASLD, EASL, ADA); calculated from routine labs; free but ~50% sensitive for F2/F3 in the key at-risk populations
- **FibroScan (VCTE)** — second-line; $150–$400 per test; 75–85% sensitivity for F2/F3 in non-obese patients; widely deployed in hepatology but scarce in primary care

**Emerging Research / Tools (2024–2026):**
- **ELF Score (Enhanced Liver Fibrosis)** — blood-based (TIMP-1, PIIINP, HA); ~75–80% sensitivity for ≥F3; Siemens Healthineers platform; increasingly validated but requires specific analyzer
- **FAST Score (FibroScan-AST)** — combines VCTE liver stiffness + controlled attenuation parameter + AST; better than FibroScan alone for MASH activity
- **MRE (Magnetic Resonance Elastography)** — most accurate NIT (~90%+ sensitivity for F2+); Resoundant platform; prohibitively expensive and scarce outside academic centers
- **AI plasma proteomics (5-protein panel)** — detects MASLD up to 16 years pre-diagnosis (76% accuracy); published in *Gastroenterology & Endoscopy News*, 2025; not yet in clinical use
- **BAST Score** — BMI + AST + T2DM + Triglycerides; newly validated against FIB-4 specifically in obese/diabetic cohorts; shows improved sensitivity for F2/F3 in primary care settings
- **AI-driven 2D Shear Wave Elastography (2D-SWE)** — filters steatosis "noise" to isolate fibrosis signal; improving upon standard FibroScan in obese patients
- **HistoIndex AI digital pathology** — quantitative image analysis of biopsy slides to reduce inter-pathologist variability in staging

**Main Limitations:**
- No single blood test reliably identifies F2 vs F3 vs F4 without imaging or biopsy
- FibroScan capital equipment cost ($30,000–$100,000) is a barrier for primary care clinics
- AI proteomic panels are research-grade; no FDA clearance yet for MASLD staging
- All NITs perform worse in the highest-risk subgroups (T2DM, Class 3 obesity, post-bariatric surgery)

### 5) Where Healthcare Is Failing (Operational Insight)

**Screening point that drops the ball:**
The primary care encounter with a T2DM or obese patient is the highest-volume missed opportunity. FIB-4 is not embedded as an automatic EHR calculation in most systems (Epic, Cerner). Even when flagged, the "indeterminate" result zone (FIB-4: 1.3–2.67) — which covers the majority of MASH patients — has **no standardized reflex protocol**. It is left to physician discretion, and in a 15-minute appointment focused on glycemia and cardiovascular risk, the liver is deprioritized every time.

**Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:**
**Automated FIB-4 flagging + reflex FibroScan order in EHR for T2DM/obese patients.** This is a software configuration change, not a new test. Hospitals with existing FibroScan units (most hepatology departments) can implement a reflex order pathway from primary care without new capital. A pilot at a single academic medical center could be operational in 30–60 days using existing Epic SmartSets or BPA (Best Practice Advisory) rules.

**High-risk population missed:**
- **T2DM patients in primary care** — 70% prevalence of MASLD, yet only 16% diagnosed; seen by PCPs, not hepatologists
- **Obese patients (BMI >35)** — FibroScan fails mechanically; no alternative NIT is routinely offered
- **Young adults (18–40) with metabolic syndrome** — MASLD is considered an "older" disease; clinicians have low index of suspicion; F2 fibrosis in a 30-year-old is almost never caught
- **Post-bariatric surgery patients** — altered anatomy confounds elastography; a population growing rapidly with no validated screening protocol

### 10) Pattern Insight (Meta-Learning)

**Recurring Diagnostic Failure Pattern Identified: "The Indeterminate Zone Trap"**

This is the third major pattern emerging across these daily briefs. The structure is always the same:

> *A screening test exists. It performs well at the extremes (rule-in at high values, rule-out at low values). But the majority of the clinically important population falls into an **indeterminate middle zone** — and the healthcare system has no standardized, automated protocol for what to do there. Physicians default to watchful waiting. Patients are lost to follow-up. The disease silently progresses.*

We saw this with **HCC** (AFP indeterminate → no reflex imaging), with **Alzheimer's** (MCI diagnosis → no biomarker reflex before p-tau217 approval), and now with **MASLD** (FIB-4 1.3–2.67 → clinical dead zone).

**Is today's disease reinforcing or breaking the pattern?**
MASLD **strongly reinforces** the pattern — and adds a new dimension: the **drug-without-a-diagnostic problem**. Resmetirom is approved, but the diagnostic infrastructure to find eligible patients (F2/F3 MASH) is broken. This is a new sub-pattern: *therapeutic approval outpacing diagnostic precision*, creating a paradox where a drug exists but cannot be appropriately prescribed at scale.

**Generalizable opportunity forming:**
Across diseases, the highest-leverage intervention is consistently **not a new biomarker** — it is **closing the indeterminate zone with an automated, reflex second-line test embedded directly into the EHR workflow**. The biomarker already exists (ELF, FibroScan, p-tau217, AFP-L3). The failure is always operational: no one automatically orders the second test when the first is inconclusive. A health-tech company or hospital QI program that systematically solves the "indeterminate zone reflex" problem — across MASLD, HCC, Alzheimer's, and PAH — is building the most generalizable and high-impact diagnostic infrastructure play of the next decade.
