Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — High-Grade Serous Ovarian Carcinoma (HGSOC) — 2026-03-30

1) Snapshot (one line)
High-Grade Serous Ovarian Carcinoma (HGSOC) — why early diagnosis fails in practice: The tumor originates in the fallopian tubes and sheds downward into the peritoneal cavity and uterus rather than the bloodstream, masking early signals behind vague, easily dismissed gastrointestinal symptoms until late-stage metastasis.

2) Why early diagnosis fails (3–5 bullets)
*   **Biological barrier:** Early lesions (Serous Tubal Intraepithelial Carcinomas, or STICs) are anatomically sequestered. By the time tumor DNA or proteins reach the systemic circulation in high enough concentrations, the cancer has already seeded the peritoneal cavity.
*   **Test limitation:** The gold-standard biomarker (CA-125) lacks early-stage sensitivity (misses ~50% of Stage I cases) and specificity (elevated by menstruation, endometriosis, and benign cysts). Transvaginal ultrasound (TVUS) yields massive false-positive rates.
*   **System failure:** The landmark UKCTOCS trial proved that population screening with CA-125/TVUS does not reduce mortality, leading guidelines to actively discourage screening. Consequently, primary care defaults to dismissing early symptoms (bloating, pelvic pain, early satiety) as IBS, UTI, or menopause.

3) Detection window & gap (concise)
*   **Earliest detectable signal (research / ideal):** STIC lesion formation and initial TP53 mutations (estimated 6.5 years before a clinical mass appears).
*   **Typical clinical detection:** Stage III/IV (widespread peritoneal dissemination and ascites).
*   **Gap to close:** 3–5 years (shifting detection from Stage III to Stage I/II increases 5-year survival from ~30% to >90%).

4) What’s being used today (gold standard + emergent)
*   **Gold standard(s):** Symptom presentation → CA-125 blood test + TVUS → Surgical excision/biopsy (no routine screening exists).
*   **Emerging research / tools:** cfDNA fragmentomics (e.g., DELFI), exosomal miRNAs, uterine lavage, and modified cervical swabs (e.g., PapSEEK) to capture tubal shedding.
*   **Main limitations:** Blood dilution of early tumor DNA (ctDNA < 0.1% in Stage I), the invasive nature of uterine lavage, and the high cost of multi-omic methylation panels.

5) Where healthcare is failing (operational insight)
*   **Screening point that drops the ball:** Primary care triage. Women >50 with new-onset GI/pelvic symptoms are routinely prescribed antacids or antispasmodics for months before a pelvic workup is initiated.
*   **Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:** Lack of automated EMR reflex testing. The threshold to trigger a basic ROMA score (CA-125 + HE4) or TVUS for persistent vague abdominal symptoms in post-menopausal women relies entirely on individual physician intuition.
*   **High-risk population missed:** BRCA1/2 mutation carriers who delay prophylactic bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (BSO) due to fertility or menopausal concerns, who currently lack reliable interim surveillance.
