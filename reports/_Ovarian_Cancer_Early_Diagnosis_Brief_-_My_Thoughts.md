Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — Ovarian Cancer (HGSC) — 2026-03-22

1) Snapshot (one line)
High-Grade Serous Ovarian Carcinoma (HGSC) — why early diagnosis fails in practice: Vague GI/pelvic symptoms are misdiagnosed as benign conditions (like IBS), while early-stage tumors shed insufficient biomarkers into the blood for reliable screening.

2) Why early diagnosis fails (3–5 bullets)
*   **Biological barrier:** Tumors typically originate in the fallopian tubes (STIC lesions) and grow silently in the spacious peritoneal cavity, remaining asymptomatic until they metastasize.
*   **Test limitation:** The primary biomarker (CA-125) is elevated in only ~50% of Stage I cases and has high false-positive rates due to benign conditions (endometriosis, menstruation).
*   **Test limitation:** Transvaginal ultrasound (TVUS) is operator-dependent and lacks the specificity to reliably distinguish benign cysts from early malignancy.
*   **System failure:** Primary care and GI workflows treat early symptoms (bloating, early satiety, urinary urgency) as functional bowel disorders or aging; the average patient sees 3+ doctors before a gynecologic oncology referral.

3) Detection window & gap (concise)
*   **Earliest detectable signal (research / ideal):** TP53 mutations or aberrant methylation in fallopian/uterine fluid, or ultra-sensitive ctDNA (1–3 years pre-symptoms).
*   **Typical clinical detection:** Stage III/IV presentation with ascites and widespread peritoneal involvement (months to years after origin).
*   **Gap to close:** 12–24 months. Catching HGSC at Stage I shifts 5-year survival from <30% to >90%.

4) What’s being used today (gold standard + emergent)
*   **Gold standard(s):** CA-125 blood test + Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVUS), followed by surgical pathology.
*   **Emerging research / tools:** Multi-cancer early detection (MCED) via ctDNA methylation, exosome surface protein profiling, and uterine/vaginal lavage fluid analysis (e.g., PapSEEK).
*   **Main limitations:** Blood volume limits ctDNA detection of small localized tumors; MCEDs lack precise tissue-of-origin specificity for adnexal masses; routine population screening is actively discouraged due to severe surgical harms from false positives.

5) Where healthcare is failing (operational insight)
*   **Screening point that drops the ball:** Primary care and gastroenterology triage. Providers fail to connect new-onset, persistent "IBS-like" symptoms in post-menopausal women to ovarian risk.
*   **Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:** Lack of algorithmic EMR prompts to reflex CA-125/TVUS for the specific high-risk symptom cluster (bloating + pelvic pain + urinary urgency > 12 days/month).
*   **High-risk population missed:** Women with unmapped BRCA1/2 or Lynch syndrome mutations, and those with strong family histories who are not enrolled in specialized high-risk surveillance programs.
