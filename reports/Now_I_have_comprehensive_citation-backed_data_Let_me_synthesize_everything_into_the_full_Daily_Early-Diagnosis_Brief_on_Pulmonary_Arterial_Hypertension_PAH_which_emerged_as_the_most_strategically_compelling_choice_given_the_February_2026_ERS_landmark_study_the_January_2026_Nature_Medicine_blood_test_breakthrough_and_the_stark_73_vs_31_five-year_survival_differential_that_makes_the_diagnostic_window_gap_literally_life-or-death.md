# Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH) — 2026-07-02

## 1) Snapshot (one line)

**Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH)** — why early diagnosis fails in practice: symptoms mimic asthma and deconditioning so convincingly that even specialist cardiologists and pulmonologists correctly diagnose it in fewer than half of simulated cases, while the confirmatory test (right heart catheterization) is ordered only after irreversible vascular remodeling is already underway.

---

## 2) Why Early Diagnosis Fails (3–5 bullets)

- **Biological barrier:** PAH produces progressive, obliterative remodeling of the pulmonary vasculature that is clinically silent until >50% of vascular reserve is lost — early symptoms (exertional dyspnea, fatigue) are indistinguishable from asthma, COPD, deconditioning, or anxiety; there is no "pain signal" to force urgent workup
- **Test limitation:** Echocardiography — the first-line screening tool — is both operator-dependent and systematically misread; a landmark February 2026 ERS/ERJ simulated-cases study across 230 European specialists found that even when echo was ordered in 92% of cases, PH was correctly identified in only **52%** of those scans; RHC (the gold standard) requires invasive catheterization at a specialist center and is rarely ordered early
- **System failure:** PAH is a rare disease (prevalence ~15–50/million) managed across three specialties (cardiology, pulmonology, rheumatology) with no unified screening protocol; primary care physicians are not trained to recognize early echo red flags (elevated TRVJ, dilated RV); over **40% of patients receive a wrong initial diagnosis** (most commonly asthma or COPD)
- **Guideline non-adherence:** The same 2026 ERS study found guideline-adherent management in only **33% of cases** — even post-diagnosis — meaning the failure is not just detection but also management pathway execution
- **High-risk populations missed:** Patients with connective tissue disease (especially systemic sclerosis/SSc), portal hypertension, congenital heart disease, and HIV are high-risk groups where annual echo screening is guideline-recommended but operationally inconsistent; CTD-associated PAH was correctly diagnosed in only **44%** of simulated specialist cases vs. 59% for idiopathic PAH

## 4) What's Being Used Today (Gold Standard + Emergent)

**Gold Standards:**
- **Right Heart Catheterization (RHC):** Definitive — measures mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP ≥20 mmHg + PVR ≥2 WU); invasive, specialist-center only, ordered late in the workup; PAWP measurement errors during RHC cause active misclassification (2024 medRxiv study)
- **Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE):** First-line screening; estimates RVSP via tricuspid regurgitation jet velocity (TRVJ); heavily operator-dependent — misread in ~48% of cases even by specialists (ERS 2026)
- **6-Minute Walk Test + NT-proBNP:** Functional and prognostic staging; NT-proBNP is standard of care for risk stratification but is a *late* signal — already elevated when significant RV dysfunction is present

**Emerging Research / Tools:**
- 🔬 **UC San Diego Blood Test** (*Nature Medicine*, Jan 9, 2026): Novel proteomics-based blood panel for early PAH detection — less invasive, potentially scalable to high-risk population screening; exact biomarker panel not yet publicly disclosed in full
- 🔬 **NOTCH3 Protein:** Elevated NOTCH3 identifies idiopathic PAH with >90% accuracy; represents a molecular upstream signal before hemodynamic compromise
- 🤖 **Us2.ai (FDA-cleared AI echo):** Automated right-heart assessment from standard echo images; eliminates human reader variability for TRVJ, RV size, and septal flattening
- 🤖 **ADOPT Trial (NCT06145880):** Active RCT validating AI-enhanced TTE for earlier PH detection in clinical settings
- 🖥️ **Temple University AI Screening Tool (June 2025):** Multimodal EHR-based algorithm flagging early PAH from routine clinical data (labs, vitals, comorbidities)
- 🧬 **Lung microbiome + metabolomic panels:** Emerging research showing dysbiotic signatures in early PAH — not yet clinical

**Main Limitations:**
- Blood test (UCSD) not yet commercially available or validated in diverse populations
- NOTCH3 data primarily from idiopathic PAH — unclear applicability to CTD-associated or other subtypes
- AI echo tools require integration into existing PACS/echo workflow — adoption friction is high
- All emerging tools lack large prospective validation in primary care or rheumatology settings where the detection opportunity is greatest

## 5) Where Healthcare Is Failing (Operational Insight)

**Screening point that drops the ball:**
The **rheumatology clinic** for systemic sclerosis (SSc) patients is the single highest-yield missed opportunity. SSc-PAH has a 10–15% lifetime prevalence and is the #1 cause of SSc-related death. Annual echo screening is guideline-recommended, but: (a) echo quality is inconsistent, (b) borderline TRVJ values (2.5–2.8 m/s) are not systematically referred, and (c) SSc patients are often managed in academic centers without co-located PH specialists.

**Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:**
**Standardized echo reporting + automatic referral trigger.** Most hospital echo labs do not have a structured field for "PH probability" in their reports. Adding a mandatory TRVJ + RV assessment field with an auto-generated referral recommendation for TRVJ >2.5 m/s is a protocol change requiring zero new technology — just a structured reporting template and an order set. This is a 30-day implementation in any hospital with an existing echo lab and EMR.

**High-risk population missed:**
- **Systemic sclerosis patients** — especially anti-centromere antibody-positive limited SSc; PH develops insidiously, often without severe lung fibrosis
- **HIV patients on ART** — PAH prevalence 0.5% (500× general population); rarely screened unless cardiopulmonary symptoms are severe
- **Patients with unexplained dyspnea + normal cardiac workup** — discharged from cardiology with "non-cardiac dyspnea" without pulmonary vascular consideration; this is the classic PAH miss in primary care and ED settings

## 6) Three High-Leverage Solution Ideas (Practical, Ranked)

### 🥇 Idea A — Structured Echo Reporting + Reflex Referral Protocol (30-day pilot)
**What:** Implement a mandatory structured echo report template that requires documentation of: (1) TRVJ value, (2) RV size (normal/mildly/moderately/severely dilated), (3) septal flattening (yes/no), (4) auto-generated "PH probability" flag (low/intermediate/high). For any intermediate/high flag, the EMR auto-generates a referral to the PH specialist team.

**How to run the 30–90 day pilot:**
- Site: Single academic hospital with echo lab + rheumatology clinic + PH specialist
- Implement structured report template in echo reporting software (GE, Philips, or Epic-native)
- Define reflex referral trigger: TRVJ ≥2.5 m/s OR RV dilation OR any 2 of 3 secondary signs
- Track: (a) % of echo reports with complete PH fields populated, (b) number of reflex referrals generated per month, (c) time from echo → RHC for referred patients, (d) new PAH diagnoses per quarter vs. historical baseline
- **Expected impact:** Literature suggests structured reporting increases PH detection rate by 30–40% in high-risk populations; pilot should demonstrate measurable reduction in time-to-diagnosis within 90 days

### 🥈 Idea B — AI-Assisted Echo Integration in Rheumatology Clinics (60–90 day pilot)
**What:** Deploy Us2.ai or equivalent FDA-cleared AI echo analysis at the point of the annual SSc echo screen in rheumatology. AI reads the echo in real-time and generates a standardized PH probability score before the rheumatologist sees the patient.

**Resource checklist:**
- [ ] Vendor agreement with Us2.ai (or equivalent: EchoGo, Caption AI)
- [ ] PACS integration or DICOM routing to AI server
- [ ] Training session for echo technicians (2 hours)
- [ ] Defined escalation pathway: AI "high probability" → same-day PH team consult
- [ ] IRB approval if collecting outcomes data for publication

**Expected impact:** Removes operator-dependent echo misread (the 48% failure rate in the ERS study); in SSc clinics, expect 2–3× increase in early PAH detection rate; publishable as a QI study within 6 months

### 🥉 Idea C — EHR-Based Pre-Symptomatic PAH Flagging Algorithm (Startup/Research)
**What:** Build and validate a machine-learning model trained on longitudinal EHR data (labs: NT-proBNP trend, CBC, PFTs; vitals: resting O₂ sat trend; ICD codes: dyspnea NOS, fatigue, SSc, HIV; medications: diuretic escalation) to generate a "PAH risk score" surfaced to primary care physicians before the patient becomes symptomatic enough to be referred.

**Highest upside:** This is the Temple University approach (June 2025) — but not yet commercialized. A startup that operationalizes this as an EHR plug-in (Epic App Orchard, Cerner marketplace) could serve every rheumatology, pulmonology, and HIV clinic in the country.

**Tests needed:**
- Retrospective validation on 5,000+ patient EHR cohort with known PAH outcomes
- Prospective pilot at 2–3 academic centers
- Head-to-head vs. current clinical practice (time-to-diagnosis as primary endpoint)

**Collaborators to approach:**
- REVEAL Registry (largest PAH registry in North America — data partnership)
- PHAROS Registry (SSc-PAH specific)

## 7) First-Principles Signal Hunt (What We Should Measure Earlier)

**Hidden signal candidate:**
**RV longitudinal strain (RVLS) on routine echo + NT-proBNP trajectory (rate of change, not absolute value).** Current practice uses single-point NT-proBNP cutoffs; but the *slope* of NT-proBNP over 6–12 months in high-risk patients (SSc, HIV, portal HTN) is a far more sensitive early warning signal than any single threshold. Similarly, RVLS deterioration precedes TRVJ elevation by 6–12 months in SSc-PAH — it is measurable on standard echo but not routinely reported.

**Second hidden signal:** NOTCH3 plasma protein — currently a research biomarker, but a simple ELISA-based assay could be incorporated into annual SSc bloodwork panels without additional phlebotomy.

**Minimal sampling change needed:**
- Add RVLS measurement to annual echo protocol in SSc/HIV/portal HTN patients (software already present in most echo machines — requires 5 minutes of additional analysis)
- Add serial NT-proBNP to annual labs in SSc patients (already drawn; just needs trending algorithm)
- No new blood draws, no new imaging modalities — just smarter use of existing data

## 10) Pattern Insight (Meta-Learning)

**What recurring diagnostic failure pattern is emerging?**

PAH reinforces a pattern that has now appeared across multiple briefs: **"Specialist-confirmed, specialist-missed."** The disease is rare enough that primary care cannot be expected to catch it — but the operational failure documented in the ERS 2026 study shows that *specialists themselves* miss it in >50% of simulated cases. This is the **"competence illusion gap"** — the assumption that routing a patient to a cardiologist or pulmonologist solves the diagnostic problem, when in fact the specialist is working with the same flawed, unstructured echo report and the same pattern-matching heuristics as a general physician.

**Is today's disease reinforcing or breaking that pattern?**
Reinforcing — and amplifying it. PAH adds a new dimension: **the 33% guideline adherence rate even post-diagnosis** means the failure is not just detection but execution. This echoes the MASLD pattern (FIB-4 exists, is underused), the AL amyloidosis pattern (FLC testing exists, is misattributed), and the Alzheimer's pattern (p-tau217 exists, is not yet in clinical workflow). The common thread: **tools exist, pathways don't.**

**Generalizable opportunity forming across diseases:**
The cross-disease meta-opportunity is **"pathway engineering over biomarker discovery."** In PAH, MASLD, AL amyloidosis, and Alzheimer's — the early biomarker either exists or is imminent. The gap is not the test; it is the **structured clinical trigger, the automated reflex order, and the specialist routing logic** that converts a biomarker value into a diagnosis. The startup/hospital improvement play that wins across all these diseases is not another biomarker — it is the **intelligent clinical decision support layer** (EHR-embedded, AI-assisted, specialty-specific) that closes the gap between "test ordered" and "diagnosis made." This is a platform play, not a point solution.
