## 2) Why Early Diagnosis Fails (3–5 bullets)

- **Biological barrier:** Amyloid fibril deposition in soft tissues (carpal tunnel, spinal ligaments, tendons) begins *a decade before* cardiac symptoms manifest — the pre-cardiac phase is entirely clinically silent from a cardiology standpoint. The heart compensates until late-stage restrictive physiology appears.
- **Test limitation:** No circulating blood biomarker exists for ATTR-CM specifically. NT-proBNP and troponin are elevated but non-specific. The gold-standard Tc-99m PYP scan (94% sensitivity, 89% specificity at Grade 2–3) is a nuclear medicine procedure not available in most community hospitals, and requires AL amyloidosis exclusion via immunofixation — a step frequently skipped, generating false-positive cascades.
- **System failure (specialty silo):** Bilateral carpal tunnel syndrome, lumbar spinal stenosis, trigger finger, and spontaneous biceps tendon rupture — classic pre-cardiac red flags — are managed entirely in orthopedic clinics with zero cardiology handoff. Hospitals do not biopsy carpal tunnel tissue during release surgeries, missing a trivially-collectible early signal.
- **System failure (HFpEF blindspot):** Only ~10% of clinicians systematically screen HFpEF patients for cardiac amyloidosis, despite ATTR-CM comprising 10–18% of HFpEF in older adults. ATTR-CM is routinely coded as "hypertensive cardiomyopathy" or "idiopathic cardiomyopathy" — 34–57% of patients are initially misdiagnosed.
- **System failure (post-HF inertia):** Even *after* a formal heart failure diagnosis, 64% of ATTR-CM patients wait >6 months for amyloid workup — median post-HF delay: **494 days (~1.4 years)**. Cardiology teams default to standard HF management without reflexively testing for reversible/treatable causes.

## 4) What's Being Used Today

**Gold Standards:**
- **Tc-99m PYP/DPD scintigraphy** — non-invasive nuclear scan; Grade 2–3 uptake: 94% sensitivity, 89% specificity; Grade 3: 100% specificity. *Requires* concurrent AL exclusion via serum/urine immunofixation + free light chains. Not widely available outside academic/tertiary centers.
- **Endomyocardial biopsy** — histological gold standard (Congo red staining + mass spectrometry for typing); invasive, reserved for ambiguous cases.
- **Echocardiography** — identifies "sparkling" myocardium, concentric LV hypertrophy, diastolic dysfunction; high sensitivity for suspicion but not diagnostic.
- **TTR gene sequencing** — mandatory to distinguish ATTRv (hereditary) from ATTRwt (wild-type); guides family screening.

**Emerging Research / Tools:**
- **AI-ECG (ECGi-ATTR model, Heart Rhythm Journal 2026):** Detects preclinical ATTR-CM from standard 12-lead ECG images with high accuracy — scalable, zero-incremental-cost screening at every cardiology visit.
- **AccurKardia (startup, 2025 patent):** AI-driven ECG software specifically patented for cardiac amyloidosis detection.
- **Attralus pan-amyloid PET radiotracer:** Quantifies *total systemic amyloid burden* (not just cardiac) — presented at ACC 2025; could enable staging and treatment monitoring.
- **Life Molecular Imaging amyloid PET radiotracer:** FDA Fast-Track designation (January 2025) for cardiac amyloid visualization.
- **Carpal tunnel tissue biopsy (opportunistic):** During routine carpal tunnel release surgery — amyloid deposits detectable by Congo red staining; a near-zero-cost add-on that could flag ATTR years before cardiac presentation.
- **Serum TTR proteomics / mass spectrometry:** Research-stage; could identify misfolded TTR variants in blood before organ deposition.

**Main Limitations:**
- PYP scan: nuclear medicine access, radiation, ~3-hour protocol, AL exclusion complexity
- AI-ECG: not yet FDA-cleared for ATTR-CM indication; needs prospective validation in diverse populations
- PET tracers: cost, availability, reimbursement — years from routine use
- No blood biomarker with adequate ATTR-CM specificity yet approved

## 5) Where Healthcare Is Failing (Operational Insight)

**Screening point that drops the ball:**
The **orthopedic-to-cardiology handoff is completely absent**. Patients with bilateral carpal tunnel syndrome (especially men >60), lumbar spinal stenosis, trigger finger, or spontaneous biceps tendon rupture are discharged from orthopedic clinics without any amyloid workup or cardiology referral. This is the single largest missed window — amyloid deposits in these tissues are present *years* before the heart is symptomatic. Carpal tunnel release surgeries occur ~500,000 times/year in the US alone; tissue biopsies are almost never sent for amyloid staining.

**Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:**
**Reflex ATTR screening protocol in HFpEF patients ≥60 years old.** Adding a structured amyloid screening checklist (red flag history + echocardiographic LVH criteria + NT-proBNP threshold) to existing HF clinic workflows — triggering automatic PYP scan ordering — requires no new technology, just protocol change and EMR order-set modification. This is a 30–60 day implementation project at any academic medical center.

**High-risk population missed:**
- **Men ≥75 with ATTRwt-CM:** Prevalence of ATTR-CM in autopsy series of octogenarians is 25%; almost none are diagnosed ante-mortem.
- **Black Americans with ATTRv-CM (Val122Ile variant):** ~3–4% of Black Americans carry this pathogenic TTR variant (estimated 1.5 million carriers in the US); this variant causes earlier-onset, more aggressive disease. Genetic screening is essentially absent in primary care.
- **Women with ATTR-CM:** Consistently underdiagnosed — present later, with more atypical features, and are underrepresented in clinical trials.

## 7) First-Principles Signal Hunt (What We Should Measure Earlier)

**Hidden signal candidate:**
**Misfolded TTR oligomers in plasma** — before fibril deposition, TTR misfolds into soluble oligomers that are measurable by mass spectrometry and emerging immunoassays. This is the pre-deposition window. Additionally, **serum retinol-binding protein 4 (RBP4)**, which circulates bound to TTR, may show altered stoichiometry as TTR begins to misfold — measurable by standard proteomics.

**Second signal:** Peripheral nerve conduction velocity changes (subclinical polyneuropathy) detectable by quantitative sensory testing years before cardiac symptoms — particularly in ATTRv carriers.

**Third signal:** **Echocardiographic global longitudinal strain (GLS) impairment** with preserved EF — GLS <−18% in a patient with LVH and no hypertension history is an underused, zero-cost trigger for amyloid workup available on any modern echo machine today.

**Minimal sampling change needed:**
- Blood (3 mL plasma) for TTR proteomics/mass spectrometry — no new collection protocol needed
- Existing carpal tunnel surgical specimens (no patient burden)
- Existing ECG data (fully retrospective mining possible)
