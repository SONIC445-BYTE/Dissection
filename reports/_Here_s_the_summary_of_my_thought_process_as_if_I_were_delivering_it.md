Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — High-Grade Serous Ovarian Cancer (HGSC) — 2026-03-18

**1) Snapshot (one line)**
High-Grade Serous Ovarian Cancer (HGSC) — early diagnosis fails because the cancer originates in the fallopian tubes and mimics common gastrointestinal distress for years before rapidly seeding the peritoneal cavity.

**2) Why early diagnosis fails (3–5 bullets)**
*   **Biological barrier:** HGSC typically begins as Serous Tubal Intraepithelial Carcinoma (STIC) in the fallopian fimbriae. These microscopic lesions shed virtually zero ctDNA into the peripheral bloodstream. 
*   **Test limitation:** The gold-standard biomarker, CA-125, lacks sensitivity for Stage I/II (elevated in only ~50% of early cases) and suffers from high false-positive rates due to benign conditions like endometriosis. Transvaginal ultrasound (TVUS) lacks the resolution to detect microscopic tubal lesions.
*   **System failure:** Primary care workflows routinely dismiss vague symptoms (bloating, early satiety, urinary frequency), routing women over 50 to gastroenterology or attributing symptoms to menopause, delaying gynecologic oncology referral by 6–12 months.
*   **Policy gap:** No approved population screening exists because massive trials (e.g., UKCTOCS) proved that annual CA-125 and TVUS do not reduce mortality, leading to a nihilistic approach to early detection.

**3) Detection window & gap (concise)**
*   **Earliest detectable signal (research / ideal):** 5–7 years pre-metastasis (the STIC lesion phase, where mutated cells shed downward into the uterine cavity).
*   **Typical clinical detection:** Stage III/IV (widespread peritoneal carcinomatosis), median 3–6 months after overt symptom onset.
*   **Gap to close:** 5+ years. Shifting detection from Stage III to Stage I increases 5-year survival from <30% to >90%.

**4) What’s being used today (gold standard + emergent)**
*   **Gold standard(s):** CA-125 blood test, Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVUS), and ultimately surgical diagnostic laparoscopy/laparotomy.
*   **Emerging research / tools:** Multi-cancer early detection (MCED) blood tests (e.g., Galleri), Pap smear genetic testing (e.g., PapSEEK), uterine lavage fluid analysis, and "smart tampons" capturing menstrual effluent for TP53 mutations.
*   **Main limitations:** MCEDs fail here—blood ctDNA fraction is too low in Stage I HGSC. Uterine lavage requires an invasive clinic visit. PapSEEK has moderate sensitivity but requires workflow changes in routine well-woman exams.

**5) Where healthcare is failing (operational insight)**
*   **Screening point that drops the ball:** Symptomatic primary care triage. Women presenting with new-onset bloating or pelvic pain over age 50 are rarely put through a standardized algorithmic pelvic workup.
*   **Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:** The lack of automated EHR reflex testing. Clinicians rely on memory to connect "persistent bloating" to "ovarian cancer risk."
*   **High-risk population missed:** BRCA1/2 mutation carriers who delay prophylactic salpingo-oophorectomy (often for family planning) and lack a reliable interim surveillance method.

**6) 3 High-leverage solution ideas (practical, ranked)**
*   **[Idea A — quick pilotable] EHR Symptom-Triggered Reflex Protocol:** Pilot a 90-day prompt in a primary care network. If a female patient >50 presents with 2+ vague symptoms (bloating, early satiety, pelvic pain) occurring >12 days/month, the EHR auto-proposes a "Pelvic Risk Panel" (CA-125 + TVUS). *Metrics to collect:* Time from symptom presentation to ultrasound completion; positive predictive value of referrals to Gyn-Onc.
*   **[Idea B — scalable tech / workflow change] At-Home Proximal Fluid Collection:** For high-risk (BRCA+) pre-menopausal women, implement a monthly molecular swab or "smart tampon" collection sent to a central lab for TP53 mutation screening. *Resource checklist:* Standardized collection kits, CLIA lab partner for targeted NGS, patient adherence tracking dashboard. *Expected impact:* Shifts detection from the blood (low signal) to the reproductive tract (high signal), creating a continuous surveillance loop.
*   **[Idea C — research / product] Microfluidic Uterine Lavage POC Device:** Develop an automated, painless, in-office uterine lavage catheter that washes the cavity and immediately filters the fluid for atypical cells/ctDNA. *Highest upside:* Replaces TVUS as the definitive surveillance tool for high-risk women. *Tests needed:* Cytology vs. molecular concordance studies. *Collaborators to approach:* Academic Gyn-Onc departments and microfluidics engineers.

**7) First-principles signal hunt (what we should measure earlier)**

*   **Minimal sampling change needed:** Shift from peripheral blood draws (low concentration/high noise) to proximal fluids: cervical mucus, menstrual effluent, or vaginal pool sampling.
