**Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC) — 2026-06-29**

---

## 1) Snapshot (one line)

**Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC)** — why early diagnosis fails in practice: Biannual ultrasound surveillance in high-risk cirrhotic patients detects only 63% of early-stage tumors, while AFP-based reflex testing misses non-secreting tumors entirely, leaving the majority of curable HCC undetected until it is surgically unresectable.

---

## 2) Why Early Diagnosis Fails (3–5 bullets)

- **Biological barrier:** HCC arises within a background of cirrhotic nodularity — the liver's own scarring creates a "visual camouflage" that masks early tumors on ultrasound; small HCC nodules (<2 cm) are morphologically indistinguishable from regenerative nodules until they reach a size that precludes curative resection.
- **Test limitation:** Ultrasound sensitivity for *early-stage* HCC is only **63%** (meta-analysis benchmark), dropping further to ~47% in obese patients and those with MASLD/NAFLD. AFP alone achieves only **39.8% sensitivity** at a fixed 90% specificity for early-stage disease. GALAD score, while better, still achieves only **38.6% sensitivity** at the same 90% specificity threshold. The newer HES V2.0 model tops out at **45.3%** — meaning over half of early-stage HCC is still missed even by the best validated panel.
- **System failure (surveillance initiation):** The most common real-world failure is **absence of surveillance altogether** — patients with known cirrhosis are not enrolled in biannual screening programs. Primary care physicians routinely fail to order abdominal ultrasounds, miss abnormal liver panels, or fail to initiate hepatitis B treatment that would lower HCC risk. Only ~20–30% of eligible cirrhotic patients in the U.S. receive guideline-concordant surveillance.
- **System failure (detection):** Even when surveillance is ordered, **up to 50% of early-stage cases escape detection** during the ultrasound visit due to operator dependency, inadequate preparation, obesity, and nodular liver texture. There is no mandatory quality threshold or real-time AI adjudication at most community hospitals.
- **System failure (population gap):** MASLD/NAFLD-associated HCC — the fastest-growing HCC etiology — disproportionately occurs in patients *without* established cirrhosis, who fall entirely outside current surveillance eligibility criteria. These patients have no screening safety net whatsoever.

## 3) Detection Window & Gap (concise)

| Signal | Timepoint |
|---|---|
| **Earliest detectable signal (research/ideal)** | HES V2.0 / HelioLiver epigenomic liquid biopsy: **up to 24 months** before clinical diagnosis; EvoLiver EV-multiomics: preclinical signal at **12–18 months** pre-diagnosis |
| **Typical clinical detection** | Most HCC diagnosed at **Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) Stage B or C** — intermediate to advanced, when 5-year survival drops from ~70% (Stage A) to <15% |

**Practical impact of closing the gap:** Stage A HCC (single tumor <5 cm, preserved liver function) is amenable to curative resection, ablation, or transplantation. Stage B/C is palliative. The 24-month detection window that HES V2.0 and HelioLiver can access, but current standard-of-care discards, is the single most valuable real estate in HCC oncology today.

## 4) What's Being Used Today (Gold Standard + Emergent)

**Gold Standards:**
- **Biannual abdominal ultrasound ± AFP** (AASLD, EASL, APASL guidelines) — the universal standard for cirrhotic patients; sensitivity 63% for early HCC; operator-dependent; no minimum quality threshold enforced
- **Multiphasic contrast-enhanced CT / MRI** — used for diagnostic confirmation after an abnormal ultrasound; high sensitivity/specificity for lesions >1 cm; not used for primary surveillance due to cost, radiation, and access constraints
- **Liver biopsy** — gold standard for ambiguous lesions; rarely needed with LI-RADS 5 imaging; carries bleeding risk in cirrhotics

**Emerging Research / Tools:**
| Tool | Type | Key Performance | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HES V2.0** | Blood panel (AFP, AFP-L3, DCP, ALT, platelets, age, etiology + longitudinal delta) | 45.3% sensitivity @ 90% specificity (early HCC); 47% sensitivity at 24 months pre-diagnosis | Phase 3 validated (Hepatology, 2025); not yet standard of care |
| **GALAD Score** | Blood panel (Gender, Age, AFP-L3, AFP, DCP) | 38.6% sensitivity @ 90% specificity; 71% sensitivity @ optimal cutoff (-1.93) | Phase 3 validated; FDA IVD submission pending |
| **HelioLiver (Helio Genomics)** | AI-powered epigenomic liquid biopsy (DNA methylation) | **76% sensitivity, 91% specificity** for early-stage HCC (CLiMB study, 2026) | Pivotal study complete; FDA submission in progress |
| **EvoLiver (Mursla Bio)** | Extracellular vesicle (EV) multiomics "Dynamic Biopsy" | Early-stage detection; quantitative EV protein + RNA profiling | **FDA Breakthrough Device Designation** (April 2025); clinical validation ongoing |
| **AI-augmented ultrasound** | Deep learning overlay on standard US | Reduces operator dependency; flags suspicious nodules in real time | Multiple academic pilots; no FDA-cleared product yet at scale |

**Main Limitations:**
- HES V2.0 and GALAD require AFP-L3 and DCP (des-gamma-carboxyprothrombin) — neither is available in most U.S. community hospitals or standard lab panels; requires send-out testing
- HelioLiver and EvoLiver are not yet commercially available; pivotal trial data is very recent
- AI-augmented ultrasound lacks standardized training data across diverse body habitus and cirrhosis etiologies

## 5) Where Healthcare Is Failing (Operational Insight)

**Screening point that drops the ball:**
The single biggest failure is at the **primary care / hepatology interface** — patients with compensated cirrhosis from NAFLD, alcohol, or hepatitis B who are managed exclusively in primary care settings are never enrolled in biannual ultrasound surveillance. There is no EMR-level automated alert that flags "cirrhosis + no HCC surveillance in 6 months = overdue." Hepatology referral is inconsistent. In safety-net hospitals, the surveillance completion rate is often below 25%.

**Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:**
**EMR-based automated surveillance tracking + opt-out scheduling.** Hospitals with Epic/Cerner already have the infrastructure. A simple "smart list" that identifies all patients with cirrhosis ICD-10 codes (K74.x), flags those with no ultrasound in the last 6 months, and auto-generates a scheduling order (opt-out framing) can be deployed in 8–12 weeks. Penn Medicine's CHTI pilot demonstrated this increases surveillance rates significantly with minimal new resources.

**High-risk population missed:**
- **MASLD/NAFLD patients without cirrhosis** — HCC risk is rising in this group but they fall outside all current surveillance guidelines; no safety net exists
- **Primary care-only cirrhotics** — no hepatology involvement; ultrasound never ordered
- **Obese patients** — standard ultrasound is technically inadequate; needs alternative modality (CEUS, MRI, or blood-based test) but no protocol switch exists
- **Immigrant populations with chronic hepatitis B** — often unaware of HCC risk; no culturally tailored outreach

## 7) First-Principles Signal Hunt (What We Should Measure Earlier)

**Hidden signal candidate:**
- **Circulating cell-free DNA methylation patterns** (the HelioLiver signal) — HCC-specific methylation marks appear in plasma before tumor mass is morphologically detectable; these are not HCC-cell-derived fragments but host epigenomic reprogramming signals that precede tumor vascularization
- **Longitudinal delta-AFP trajectory** — not the absolute AFP value, but the *rate of change* over 6-month intervals; a rising AFP trajectory (even within "normal" range) is a stronger early signal than a single threshold cross; currently ignored in standard practice
- **Platelet count trajectory** — falling platelet count in a cirrhotic patient signals portal hypertension progression and correlates with HCC risk escalation; already in every CBC but never used as a surveillance trigger

**Minimal sampling change needed:**
- For HES V2.0: **standard blood draw** (serum) — AFP, AFP-L3, DCP, ALT, platelet count + patient age/etiology already available; zero new sample type required; only lab panel expansion needed
- For HelioLiver/EvoLiver: **standard EDTA blood draw** — same venipuncture as routine labs; no special collection or processing required at point of care

**Key insight:** The data needed to detect HCC 12–24 months earlier is largely *already being drawn* during routine cirrhosis monitoring visits — it's just not being measured or computed correctly.

## 10) Pattern Insight (Meta-Learning)

**Recurring diagnostic failure pattern emerging across briefs:**

Today's HCC brief reinforces a pattern that is crystallizing across high-mortality diseases: **"The surveillance system exists on paper but fails at execution."** The biological signal is detectable earlier than we act on it. The test exists (or nearly exists). The patient is already in the healthcare system. And yet the diagnosis is still delayed by 12–24 months — not because of a missing scientific breakthrough, but because of a **workflow execution gap** at the primary care / specialist interface.

This is the same pattern seen in:
- **Pancreatic cancer** — high-risk patients (familial PDAC, new-onset diabetes) are known but not enrolled in EUS/MRI surveillance
- **Alzheimer's disease** — plasma p-tau217 is now validated, but primary care has no protocol for who to test or what to do with a positive result
- **Endometriosis** — the diagnostic criteria are known; the delay is driven by symptom dismissal and lack of a non-invasive triage tool

**The generalizable opportunity forming:**
The next wave of high-impact diagnostic companies will not be built on discovering new biomarkers — they will be built on **closing the surveillance execution gap**: automated patient identification from EHR data, opt-out scheduling infrastructure, reflex lab panel integration, and population-level blood-first screening that removes operator dependency and specialist access barriers. The scientific signal exists. The commercial and clinical opportunity is in the **last mile of delivery**.

The companies that build the *infrastructure layer* — connecting validated biomarker panels to cirrhotic registries, risk-stratified outreach, and automated follow-up — will capture disproportionate value, because they solve the problem that neither pharma nor academic medicine has incentive to fix.
