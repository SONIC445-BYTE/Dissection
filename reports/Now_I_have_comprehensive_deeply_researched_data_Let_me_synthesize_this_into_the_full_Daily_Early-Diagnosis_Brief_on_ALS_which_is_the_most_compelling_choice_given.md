## Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) — 2026-07-25

---

### 1) Snapshot (one line)

**Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)** — early diagnosis fails because it is a clinical *diagnosis of exclusion* anchored to observing progressive neurodegeneration, structurally baking a 10–16 month delay into the pathway while validated blood biomarkers (pNfH, NfL) sit unused in most primary care and general neurology settings.

---

### 2) Why Early Diagnosis Fails (3–5 bullets)

- **Biological barrier:** ALS has no pathognomonic early symptom — initial fasciculations, focal limb weakness, or subtle dysarthria are non-specific and mimic radiculopathy, cervical myelopathy, multifocal motor neuropathy, and Kennedy disease. The disease is invisible until multi-region motor neuron loss is observable — by definition, late.

- **Test limitation (criteria-embedded delay):** The revised El Escorial Criteria (now superseded by Gold Coast Criteria as of 2025) required progressive UMN + LMN signs in *multiple* body regions before diagnosis — meaning clinicians were mandated to *watch the disease spread* before acting. Even with Gold Coast Criteria (now requiring only one region), EMG remains the confirmatory tool, with typical EMG-to-suspected-diagnosis delays of **2.1 months** and EMG-to-definitive-diagnosis delays of **2.9 months** post-test.

- **Biomarker underutilization:** Plasma pNfH (phosphorylated neurofilament heavy chain) achieves **97.3% sensitivity and 83.8–94.2% specificity** for ALS vs. mimics — yet as of mid-2026, it is clinically available at only a handful of centers (Mayo Clinic Labs, WashU ALS Center). No standardized reflex-ordering protocol exists in primary care or general neurology EMRs.

- **System failure — specialist access gap:** General neurologists take **twice as long** to diagnose ALS as ALS specialist centers. Yet most patients never reach an ALS center until after a prolonged general neurology odyssey. Limb-onset patients (~70% of cases) are most commonly sent first to orthopedics or spine surgery — not neurology — creating a systematic triage misfire.

- **System failure — primary care knowledge gap:** Self-reported PCP surveys confirm diagnostic uncertainty and limited ALS clinical knowledge as the #1 driver of delay. ~50% of patients over age 60 are initially misdiagnosed. There is no EMR-embedded decision support tool in widespread use to flag the ALS phenotype at the primary care level.

### 3) Detection Window & Gap

| Stage | Signal | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Earliest detectable (research)** | Plasma pNfH / NfL elevation; ML-detectable blood biomarker signatures | Up to **10 years** before symptom onset (ML models, 2025 data) |
| **Preclinical biomarker window** | Elevated serum NfL in familial ALS (fALS) gene carriers | **6–24 months** pre-symptom |
| **Symptom onset to first consult** | Patient self-delay | +3–6 months |
| **First consult to diagnosis** | Clinical/EMG pathway | +6–12 months |
| **Typical clinical detection** | Progressive multi-region motor signs confirmed by EMG | **10–16 months** post-symptom onset |
| **Gap to close** | ~10–14 months of actionable diagnostic time lost | **Half the median survival window** (median ALS survival: 2–5 years) |

**Practical impact of the gap:** Every month of diagnostic delay is a month without access to riluzole, edaravone, AMX0035, or emerging gene therapies (tofersen for SOD1-ALS). For fast progressors (high NfL at baseline), delay is existential — they may lose ventilator-free survival time, eligibility for clinical trials, and the ability to complete advance care planning.

### 4) What's Being Used Today (Gold Standard + Emergent)

**Gold Standards:**
- **Clinical examination** — progressive UMN + LMN signs (Gold Coast Criteria, 2025 revision)
- **EMG/Nerve Conduction Studies (NCS)** — detects active and chronic denervation; confirmatory but not early
- **MRI brain/spine** — primarily used to *exclude* mimics (cervical myelopathy, brain tumors), not to diagnose ALS
- **Genetic testing** — SOD1, C9orf72, FUS, TARDBP for familial ALS (~10% of cases); increasingly offered to all patients

**Emerging / High-Signal Tools (2025–2026):**
| Tool | Status | Performance |
|---|---|---|
| **Serum pNfH (phospho-NfH)** | Clinically available (Mayo, WashU) | Sensitivity 97.3%, Specificity 83.8–94.2% |
| **Serum NfL (Quanterix Simoa)** | Clinically available, broader access | Sensitivity >80–85%; less ALS-specific than pNfH |
| **ML blood biomarker panels** | Research / early validation | Detects ALS signatures up to 10 years pre-symptom |
| **DiSPAH (AI progression model)** | Research (June 2026) | Predicts progression rate and functional failure sequence |
| **Neurodex AI platform** | Early commercial (Canada) | AI blood profiling for early ALS detection |
| **ALS.ai** | Startup / drug-discovery + diagnostic | End-to-end AI pipeline |
| **Speech/motor digital biomarkers** | Research | Detects subtle bulbar/motor changes pre-diagnosis |

**Main Limitations:**
- pNfH/NfL are **non-specific** for ALS alone (elevated in any neuroaxonal injury) — require clinical context
- No FDA-cleared, standalone blood test for ALS diagnosis exists as of July 2026
- ML presymptomatic models validated primarily in familial ALS cohorts — generalizability to sporadic ALS (90% of cases) unproven at scale
- EMG remains operator-dependent and access-limited in rural/low-resource settings

### 5) Where Healthcare Is Failing (Operational Insight)

**Screening point that drops the ball:**
> **Primary care and orthopedics** — the first clinical contact for ~70% of limb-onset ALS patients. PCPs and orthopedic surgeons lack EMR-embedded ALS phenotype flags. Patients with painless progressive weakness + fasciculations + absent sensory deficit are booked for cervical spine MRI and physio, not neurofilament blood draws or urgent neurology referral.

**Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:**
> **Reflex pNfH/NfL ordering protocol** embedded in EMR for patients ≥45 years presenting with unexplained progressive painless weakness, fasciculations, or dysarthria — with automatic fast-track neurology/ALS-center referral if elevated. This requires: (1) lab partnership with Mayo or WashU for send-out testing, (2) a single EMR alert rule, (3) a referral pathway agreement with the nearest ALS center. Zero new infrastructure needed.

**High-risk population most missed:**
> **Limb-onset ALS patients aged 55–75** — the largest demographic, most likely to be triaged to orthopedics or spine surgery. Patients scheduled for cervical decompression surgery who lack sensory deficits but have upper limb fasciculations and atrophy are a critical interception point. Also: **bulbar-onset women**, who are frequently referred to ENT or gastroenterology for dysarthria/dysphagia before neurology.

### 7) First-Principles Signal Hunt (What We Should Measure Earlier)

**Hidden signal candidate:**
> **Plasma pNfH trajectory (rate of rise, not absolute value)** — a single elevated pNfH is sensitive but not specific; a *rising slope* over 2–3 serial draws in a presymptomatic carrier is far more specific for impending ALS onset. Combine with **digital speech biomarkers** (subtle articulatory timing changes detectable by smartphone mic before clinical dysarthria) and **wearable motor variability metrics** (tremor/fasciculation frequency captured by accelerometer). This multi-modal signal composite is the earliest detectable fingerprint.

**Minimal sampling change needed:**
> A single **blood draw + 2-minute smartphone speech sample** at primary care visit — no lumbar puncture, no PET scan, no EMG required for initial triage. The blood draw is standard venipuncture; the speech sample uses validated apps (e.g., from the Harvard ALS Biomarker Study). This is a zero-friction, zero-infrastructure-change addition to any annual physical.
