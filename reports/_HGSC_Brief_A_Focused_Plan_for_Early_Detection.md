1) Snapshot (one line)
High-Grade Serous Ovarian Carcinoma (HGSC) — why early diagnosis fails in practice: We screen the ovaries and systemic blood for a cancer that actually originates in the fallopian tubes and sheds locally into the uterus.

2) Why early diagnosis fails (3–5 bullets)
*   **Biological barrier:** HGSC begins as microscopic lesions (STICs) in the fallopian tube fimbriae, not the ovary. By the time it forms an ovarian mass, it has often already seeded the peritoneal cavity. 
*   **Test limitation:** The gold standard blood biomarker, CA-125, is highly non-specific (elevated by endometriosis, menstruation, cysts) and requires a high tumor burden to trigger. Transvaginal ultrasound (TVUS) lacks the resolution to detect microscopic tubal precursors.
*   **System failure:** Primary care routinely misinterprets early, vague symptoms (persistent bloating, early satiety, GI distress) as IBS or menopause, leading to months of irrelevant GI workups before a GYN oncology referral.
*   **Physics of dilution:** Early-stage tumor DNA shed into the bloodstream (ctDNA) is diluted to near-zero, making standard liquid biopsies highly prone to false negatives in Stage I/II.

3) Detection window & gap (concise)
*   **Earliest detectable signal (research / ideal):** *TP53* mutations and tumor DNA in uterine lavage fluid or vaginal tampons (months to years before mass formation).
*   **Typical clinical detection:** Stage III/IV (severe ascites, widespread peritoneal metastasis).
*   **Gap to close:** 2–4 years. Closing this gap shifts the 5-year survival rate from <30% to >90%.

4) What’s being used today (gold standard + emergent)
*   **Gold standard(s):** CA-125 blood test + Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVUS) (proven ineffective for population screening by the UKCTOCS trial).
*   **Emerging research / tools:** Uterine lavage genomic analysis (e.g., Mass General research), specialist medical tampons for capturing shed cells (e.g., Southampton trial), fragmentomics (DELFI), and exosome-based blood tests (e.g., UQ's recent 500-sample validation).
*   **Main limitations:** Blood-based methods still fight the dilution problem; uterine lavage requires an in-office clinical procedure; tampon methods are still in clinical validation for standardized DNA extraction.

*   **High-risk population missed:** Women with no known family history (who make up ~80% of cases) and BRCA1/2 carriers who delay prophylactic oophorectomy due to the desire to preserve fertility or avoid early menopause.
