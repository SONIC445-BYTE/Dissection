### 1) Snapshot (one line)

**Endometriosis** — why early diagnosis fails in practice: a decade-long diagnostic odyssey persists because the only definitive test is invasive surgery, non-invasive biomarkers have historically been unreliable, and primary care systematically normalizes severe pelvic pain as "just bad periods."

---

### 2) Why Early Diagnosis Fails (5 bullets)

- **Biological barrier:** Endometriosis lesions produce a heterogeneous, stage-independent inflammatory milieu — peritoneal IL-6, VEGF, and immune cell signatures are real but highly variable across individuals and menstrual cycle phases, making single-marker blood tests unreliable without multi-omic panels
- **Test limitation:** CA-125 — the most widely ordered blood marker — has <30% sensitivity for early-stage (I/II) disease and ~70% sensitivity only in advanced Stage III/IV; no FDA-cleared non-invasive test existed until 2025/2026; imaging (TVUS, MRI) routinely misses superficial peritoneal lesions
- **Gold standard bottleneck:** Diagnostic laparoscopy (gold standard) costs $12,000–$20,000 in the US, requires general anesthesia, carries surgical risk, and generates waiting lists of up to **69 weeks** in public health systems (UK data) — making it completely unsuitable as a population-level screening tool
- **System failure — normalization:** Primary care physicians, emergency departments, and even gynecologists routinely attribute dysmenorrhea and pelvic pain to stress, IBS, or PID; patients average **4–7 healthcare provider visits** before receiving a correct diagnosis; symptom dismissal is documented across all healthcare systems globally
- **System failure — referral pathway:** Insurance pre-authorizations for pelvic MRI and specialist backlogs add 6–12 months after GP referral; there is no structured endometriosis triage pathway in most hospital systems — patients fall into a GI/GYN/ER loop for years

### 4) What's Being Used Today (Gold Standard + Emergent)

**Gold Standard(s):**
- **Diagnostic laparoscopy + histopathology** — definitive but invasive, expensive, skill-dependent, and creates 12–18 month surgical queue delays
- **Transvaginal ultrasound (TVUS)** — useful for endometriomas and deep infiltrating lesions; misses >60% of superficial peritoneal disease
- **CA-125 blood test** — widely ordered but clinically inadequate; sensitivity <30% Stage I/II; only modestly elevated in advanced disease

**Emerging Research / Tools (2025–2026):**
- 🔬 **PromarkerEndo** (Proteomics International, Australia): 10-plasma-protein panel, **83% sensitivity / 95% specificity** across all stages; 704-participant clinical validation; commercial launch Australia H2 2025, US pathway in progress
- 🔬 **HerResolve** (HerAnova Lifesciences): Multi-omic assay — 3 serum miRNAs + 3 proteins + 1 steroid hormone + age + BMI; Random Forest ML algorithm; **AUC 0.944, 80% sensitivity / 97.5% specificity**; identified **61.5% of cases missed by TVUS/MRI**; multi-center validation published March 2026
- 🔬 **Ziwig Endotest** (salivary miRNA + AI): **>96% diagnostic accuracy** in NEJM Evidence clinical trial; available via clinical trial framework in Europe since February 2025 — the most disruptive non-invasive signal yet
- 🔬 **Urine proteomics + ML** (Genetic Algorithm models): pilot data showing **96.7% sensitivity / 93.5% specificity**
- 🔬 **Menstrual blood lipidome assay**: 81% sensitivity / 85% specificity in proof-of-concept
- 🤖 **AI symptom checkers** (e.g., Doctronic): estimated to reduce diagnostic delay by **>4 years** and save **>$5,000/patient**; ML models (Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM) on miRNA/genetic data achieving pooled AUC **0.982**, 94% sensitivity / 99% specificity (2025 meta-analysis, *Frontiers in Endocrinology*)
- 🤖 **Deep learning on TVUS** (ResNet-152): AUC 0.986 for differentiating endometriomas from other ovarian cysts

**Main Limitations:**
- PromarkerEndo and HerResolve not yet FDA-cleared (regulatory pathway 2026+)
- Ziwig Endotest: European clinical trial only; saliva collection standardization needed
- Urine/menstrual blood assays: no multi-center validation yet
- All ML models require diverse training cohorts — current data skewed toward European/White populations

### 5) Where Healthcare is Failing (Operational Insight)

**Screening point that drops the ball:** The **primary care first visit** — the GP appointment where a patient first reports dysmenorrhea, chronic pelvic pain, or dyspareunia. In the absence of a non-invasive triage test, GPs have no objective tool to differentiate endometriosis from functional pain, IBS, or PID. The default is empirical treatment (NSAIDs, OCPs) for 1–3 years before referral — this is where the bulk of the 7-year delay is manufactured.

**Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:**
- **Implement structured endometriosis triage at GP level**: A symptom-scoring tool (e.g., validated Endometriosis Symptom Inventory or digital AI checker) combined with an expedited gynecology referral protocol for patients scoring above threshold. No new technology needed — just a workflow protocol change. Hospitals with gynecology departments can implement this in a single training cycle.
- **Reflex CA-125 + pelvic MRI order pathway**: For patients with ≥2 visits for pelvic pain in 12 months, auto-trigger an endometriosis pathway rather than waiting for patient advocacy.

**High-risk population missed:**
- **Adolescents (ages 13–21):** Severe dysmenorrhea in teenagers is almost universally attributed to "normal periods." Studies show 60–70% of adolescents with chronic pelvic pain have endometriosis at laparoscopy — yet average time to diagnosis in this group is the longest (up to 11+ years). No current screening protocol exists for this age group.
- **Women with IBS diagnosis:** ~40% of women diagnosed with IBS may have undetected endometriosis — the GI pathway completely silos these patients away from gynecology.

### 7) First-Principles Signal Hunt (What We Should Measure Earlier)

**Hidden signal candidate:** **Salivary and serum microRNA panels** — specifically miRNA signatures reflecting systemic immune dysregulation (upregulated miR-200b, miR-141, let-7 family alterations documented in endometriosis). The key insight: endometriosis is a systemic inflammatory disease, not just a local pelvic one. Peripheral blood and saliva carry immune cell-derived exosomal miRNAs that reflect ectopic lesion activity long before imaging becomes positive. Additionally, **peritoneal immune cell signatures** (NK cell dysfunction, macrophage M2 polarization markers) are detectable in serum before clinical symptoms are severe enough to trigger referral.

**Second candidate:** **Urine metabolomics / lipidome** — pilot data shows menstrual blood lipidome and urine proteomics can identify endometriosis with high accuracy; these are completely non-invasive and could be self-collected at home, enabling population-level screening.

**Minimal sampling change needed:**
- Saliva (Ziwig Endotest model): self-collected at home, postal kit — zero clinical infrastructure needed
- Venous blood draw (PromarkerEndo/HerResolve): standard phlebotomy at GP visit — no specialist required
- Urine: self-collected, completely frictionless — ideal for adolescent screening programs

### 10) Pattern Insight (Meta-Learning)

**Recurring diagnostic failure pattern:** This brief reinforces what is now the **dominant pattern** across multiple diseases analyzed: **the gold standard test is the bottleneck, not the disease biology.** In ALS, the bottleneck is the exclusion-based neurological workup. In pancreatic cancer, it's CT sensitivity for small lesions. In endometriosis, it's laparoscopy. In all three cases, the biology was *detectable earlier* — the system just had no non-invasive, primary-care-deployable triage test to act on that signal.

**Is today's disease reinforcing or breaking the pattern?** **Strongly reinforcing** — but with a critical new dimension: endometriosis adds the **sociocultural normalization layer**. Unlike ALS or PDAC (where the delay is purely technical/biological), endometriosis delay is *manufactured* by a healthcare culture that dismisses women's pain. This means the solution requires **both** a technical fix (non-invasive biomarker test) and a **workflow/cultural fix** (GP training, triage protocols, adolescent screening programs). Neither alone is sufficient.

**Generalizable opportunity forming across diseases:** The convergence of **multi-omic liquid biopsies + lightweight ML classifiers + self-collection sampling (saliva/urine/blood)** is creating a new category: **"GP-deployable triage tests"** that can be ordered at the first clinical contact — before specialist referral, before imaging, before invasive procedures. The startup opportunity is not in building one test for one disease — it is in building the **platform infrastructure** (saliva/blood collection kits → multi-disease ML classifier → structured EMR referral trigger) that can triage endometriosis, ovarian cancer, ALS, and rare diseases from a single primary care touchpoint. The disease that cracks this model first wins the platform.
