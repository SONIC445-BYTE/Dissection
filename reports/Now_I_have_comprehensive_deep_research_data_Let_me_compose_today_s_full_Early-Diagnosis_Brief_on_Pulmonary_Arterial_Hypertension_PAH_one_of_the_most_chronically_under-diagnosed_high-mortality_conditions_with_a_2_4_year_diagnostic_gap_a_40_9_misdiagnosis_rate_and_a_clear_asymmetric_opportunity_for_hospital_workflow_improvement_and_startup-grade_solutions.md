## 1) Snapshot (one line)

**Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH)** — why early diagnosis fails in practice: Exertional dyspnea is reflexively attributed to asthma, COPD, deconditioning, or anxiety for 2–4 years before right heart strain becomes unmistakable on echocardiography — by which point irreversible vascular remodeling has already occurred.

---

## 2) Why Early Diagnosis Fails (3–5 bullets)

- **Biological barrier:** PAH is a progressive obliterative vasculopathy — the pulmonary vasculature loses ~50–60% of its reserve before resting hemodynamics become abnormal. Early symptoms (exertional dyspnea, fatigue, near-syncope) are nonspecific and episodic, with no pain signal to trigger urgency.
- **Test limitation:** Echocardiography — the de facto first-line screen — misses early-stage PAH in a substantial fraction of patients; TR velocity estimation is unreliable in poor acoustic windows, and mild right ventricular strain is routinely under-called. NT-proBNP is not sensitive enough for early disease and is confounded by renal impairment (falsely elevated in CKD, masking interpretation).
- **Diagnostic anchoring:** 40.9% of PAH patients are initially misdiagnosed — most commonly as COPD (10.1%), asthma, or anxiety. Even specialist simulation studies show cardiologists and pulmonologists missing the diagnosis >50% of the time when respiratory overlap exists.
- **Gold standard gatekeeping:** Right Heart Catheterization (RHC) — the only definitive diagnostic test — is invasive, requires a cath lab, and is ordered only after multiple non-invasive tests show advanced disease. Primary care and general cardiologists will not refer for RHC until echocardiography is already abnormal.
- **System failure:** Annual PAH screening is mandated by guidelines for high-risk populations (systemic sclerosis, MCTD) but is poorly implemented in rheumatology practices — DLCO, PFTs, and echo are frequently not co-ordered. The DETECT algorithm (validated, highly sensitive for SSc-PAH) remains largely unused outside specialist PAH centers.

## 4) What's Being Used Today (Gold Standard + Emergent)

**Gold Standards:**
- **Right Heart Catheterization (RHC):** Definitive — mPAP >20 mmHg + PVR ≥2 Wood Units at rest. Invasive, cath-lab dependent, ordered too late in practice.
- **Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE):** First-line non-invasive screen. Estimates RVSP via TR jet velocity. Sensitivity for early/mild PAH is poor; operator-dependent; misses 20–30% of confirmed PAH cases at early stages.
- **NT-proBNP / BNP:** Risk stratification tool, not a screening biomarker. Confounded by renal function.
- **6-Minute Walk Test (6MWT) + DLCO:** Used in high-risk populations (SSc); DLCO decline precedes symptoms and is underutilized as a trigger for echo referral.

**Emerging Research / Tools:**
- **DETECT Algorithm (2-step):** Uses FVC/DLCO ratio, anticentromere antibodies, NT-proBNP, serum urate, ECG right-axis deviation (Step 1) → RA area + TR velocity (Step 2). Validated for SSc-PAH; significantly reduces missed diagnoses vs. echo-alone screening. *Underused outside specialist centers.*
- **AI-powered echocardiography (Us2.ai PANACEA-HF; multimodal deep learning — Nature 2025):** Automated RV strain detection from standard echo loops; enables non-expert acquisition and AI-read in primary care.
- **Eko Health AI stethoscope + algorithm:** FDA-cleared; flags PH risk signals from auscultation + ECG in primary care.
- **NT-proCNP (Nature Scientific Reports, 2025):** Novel biomarker being evaluated specifically for PAH in respiratory-overlap patients — may outperform BNP in discriminating PAH from COPD-related dyspnea.
- **Cardiac MRI (CMR):** Gold-standard RV assessment; detects subtle RV remodeling before echo changes, but cost/access limits routine use.
- **Circulating endothelin-1 / microRNA panels:** Research phase; promising for pre-symptomatic detection in BMPR2 mutation carriers.

**Main Limitations:** AI echo tools not yet integrated into primary care EHR workflows; DETECT algorithm not embedded in rheumatology EMRs; NT-proCNP not commercially available; CMR not scalable.

## 5) Where Healthcare Is Failing (Operational Insight)

**Screening point that drops the ball:**
The **primary care / general internal medicine visit** — where patients with exertional dyspnea are seen 3–5 times over 1–2 years before any cardiac or pulmonary referral. Spirometry is ordered (showing normal or mild obstruction), COPD or anxiety is diagnosed, and inhaled bronchodilators are prescribed. No one orders a DLCO or an echo at this stage.

**Second critical failure:** Rheumatology clinics managing scleroderma patients — annual PAH screening (PFT + echo + DLCO) is guideline-mandated but inconsistently executed. The DETECT algorithm, which would catch SSc-PAH earlier and more reliably, is not embedded in rheumatology EMR order sets anywhere at scale.

**Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:**
→ **Embedding a DETECT Step 1 auto-calculator into rheumatology EMR order sets** for all SSc/MCTD patients. Step 1 uses only blood tests + ECG + PFT data already in the chart — zero new tests required. A positive Step 1 auto-triggers an echo referral with "PAH rule-out" priority. This is a **pure workflow change** — no new equipment, no new budget.

**High-risk population most missed:**
- **Systemic Sclerosis (SSc) patients in general rheumatology** — PAH develops in 8–12% of SSc patients; it is the leading cause of SSc mortality. Yet most are managed by community rheumatologists who do not run annual DETECT screens.
- **Young women (20–45) with unexplained exertional dyspnea** — consistently diagnosed with anxiety/deconditioning for 18+ months before PAH is considered.
- **HIV-positive patients** — PAH prevalence 0.5% in HIV (2,500× general population risk); not systematically screened at HIV clinics.

## 7) First-Principles Signal Hunt (What We Should Measure Earlier)

**Hidden signal candidate:**
**DLCO decline rate** — the single most underutilized early PAH signal in clinical practice. DLCO (diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide) falls early in PAH due to reduced pulmonary capillary bed surface area — often 12–24 months before echocardiographic TR velocity becomes abnormal. A DLCO <70% predicted in a scleroderma patient with FVC/DLCO ratio >1.6 is a DETECT Step 1 trigger — yet this data sits unused in PFT reports filed away in the chart.

**Secondary signal:** Serum urate elevation (a marker of anaerobic metabolism from low cardiac output) — already part of DETECT Step 1, cheap, universally available, and almost never interpreted in the context of PAH risk.

**Minimal sampling change needed:**
- Blood (serum urate, NT-proBNP/NT-proCNP) — already drawn in most rheumatology/cardiology visits
- Existing PFT reports (DLCO) — already in EHR, just not algorithmically parsed
- ECG (right-axis deviation) — already done, just not flagged for PAH risk
- **Zero new invasive tests required for Step 1 DETECT** — the data is already there; the problem is pure algorithmic/workflow failure.

## 10) Pattern Insight (Meta-Learning)

**Recurring diagnostic failure pattern emerging:**

Today's PAH brief reinforces a pattern now seen across **3+ consecutive disease categories** in this series (PAH, NASH/MASLD, SLE, Ovarian Cancer):

> **The "Assembled Data" Failure Pattern:** The earliest diagnostic signals already exist in the patient's chart — they are being measured, recorded, and filed. The failure is not a missing test. It is the absence of an algorithm that *assembles* existing data points into a risk score and triggers a workflow action. DLCO is in the PFT report. Serum urate is in the metabolic panel. Right-axis deviation is on the ECG. NT-proBNP is in the cardiology note. None of these are connected.

**Is today's disease reinforcing or breaking the pattern?**
**Strongly reinforcing.** PAH is arguably the *purest* example of this pattern — DETECT Step 1 requires zero new tests and is 100% derivable from existing chart data. This is not a biomarker gap; it is a *data orchestration* gap.

**Generalizable opportunity forming:**
The cross-disease startup opportunity is becoming clear: **a disease-agnostic "latent risk aggregator"** — an EHR-embedded CDS layer that continuously scans existing lab/imaging/ECG data for pre-specified multi-marker risk signatures (DETECT for PAH, FIB-4 for NASH, complement + ANA pattern for SLE) and surfaces actionable alerts before the patient becomes symptomatic enough to trigger a specialist visit. This is not a diagnostics company — it is a **clinical intelligence layer** sitting on top of existing EHR data. The moat is the validated algorithm library, not the hardware.
