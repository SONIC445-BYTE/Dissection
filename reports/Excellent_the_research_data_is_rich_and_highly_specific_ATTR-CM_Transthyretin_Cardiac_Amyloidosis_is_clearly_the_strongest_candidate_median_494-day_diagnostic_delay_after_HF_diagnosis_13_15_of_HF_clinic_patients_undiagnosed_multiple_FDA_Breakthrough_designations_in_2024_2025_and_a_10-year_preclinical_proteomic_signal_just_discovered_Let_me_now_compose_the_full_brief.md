## 2) Why Early Diagnosis Fails (3–5 bullets)

- **Biological barrier:** ATTR-CM is a slow-onset infiltrative cardiomyopathy — misfolded transthyretin protein deposits silently stiffen the myocardium over years. Early symptoms (exertional dyspnea, fatigue, mild edema) are clinically indistinguishable from the far more common HFpEF, hypertensive cardiomyopathy, or age-related diastolic dysfunction. The disease "hides in plain sight" inside a massive, poorly-differentiated diagnostic category.

- **Test limitation:** The gold-standard PYP (pyrophosphate) scintigraphy scan is highly sensitive and non-invasive — but it is *never ordered* without prior clinical suspicion. Standard heart failure workups (echo, BNP, ECG) can appear consistent with multiple diagnoses. The classic ECG finding (low-voltage + LVH paradox) is present in only ~50% of cases and is routinely missed. NT-proBNP and troponin are elevated but non-specific.

- **System failure (awareness):** ATTR-CM diagnosis is *entirely suspicion-dependent* — there is no automated screening trigger in any EHR or cardiology workflow. A 2025 Medicare study found the median time from heart failure diagnosis to ATTR-CM diagnosis was **494 days** (IQR: 63–1,340 days), with **64% of patients experiencing a delay >6 months**. Cardiologists at non-specialist centers have low index of suspicion.

- **System failure (equity):** Hereditary ATTR (Val122Ile variant) disproportionately affects Black Americans (~3–4% carrier frequency), yet Black patients face *longer* diagnostic delays due to lower referral rates to specialty centers and geographic inaccessibility of nuclear imaging.

- **Missed extracardiac signals:** ATTR-CM has highly predictive prodromal features — bilateral carpal tunnel syndrome (precedes cardiac diagnosis by **5–7 years** on average), lumbar spinal stenosis, biceps tendon rupture, and peripheral neuropathy — yet these are managed by orthopedic surgeons and neurologists who rarely connect them to cardiac amyloid risk.
