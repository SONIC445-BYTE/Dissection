## Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) — 2026-06-19

---

### 1) Snapshot (one line)

**ALS** — why early diagnosis fails in practice: A 12–16 month diagnostic delay consumes **20–50% of a patient's entire survival window**, caused by symptom heterogeneity, primary care misattribution, and the absence of a validated, widely-deployed blood-based test despite multiple near-ready candidates.

---

### 2) Why Early Diagnosis Fails (3–5 bullets)

- **Biological barrier:** ALS presents with highly variable initial symptoms — foot drop, dysarthria, fasciculations, hand weakness — that mimic radiculopathy, orthopedic injury, or stroke. No single early symptom pattern is pathognomonic. Pre-symptomatic neuronal death of up to **80% of motor neurons** may occur before clinical detection thresholds are reached.
- **Test limitation:** The gold standard (EMG + clinical criteria) requires observable upper AND lower motor neuron (UMN/LMN) signs across body regions — by definition, a *late-stage* requirement. Blood biomarkers NfL, pNfH, GFAP, and EV-TDP-43 are validated in research settings but **not yet integrated into routine hospital diagnostic pathways**. The Johns Hopkins 2025 blood test (>98% accuracy) and Brain Chemistry Labs' rapid assay (97% accuracy) remain outside standard clinical protocols.
- **Criteria lag:** El Escorial and Awaji criteria — still used in many hospitals — require multi-region progression before confirming "definite ALS." The newer **Gold Coast Criteria (GCC)**, validated in 2024–2025 with significantly higher sensitivity, allows diagnosis from LMN dysfunction in just two regions, yet adoption is inconsistent globally.
- **System failure:** Primary care physicians — who see patients first — have low ALS pattern recognition. A Cleveland Clinic survey identified **PCP misattribution** (to orthopedics, spine, or psychiatric causes) as the #1 cause of delay. Specialist wait times at tertiary neurology centers add a further 3–6 months. There is no EMG-first reflex protocol in primary care.
- **Equity gap:** Racial disparities compound delays — Black patients in the US experience significantly longer diagnostic delays and present with worse respiratory function and greater disease severity at time of diagnosis (PubMed 37909302), reflecting both access barriers and differential referral patterns.

### 4) What's Being Used Today (Gold Standard + Emergent)

**Gold Standards:**
- **Needle EMG** — detects active/chronic denervation; the cornerstone of ALS workup but operator-dependent, uncomfortable, and requires specialist access
- **Clinical criteria application** — Gold Coast Criteria (GCC) now preferred over Revised El Escorial / Awaji; requires progressive UMN + LMN signs across ≥1 body region (GCC) vs. multiple regions (rEEC)
- **MRI brain/spine** — used to rule out structural mimics, not to confirm ALS
- **Nerve conduction studies (NCS)** — complement EMG

**Emerging Research / Tools:**
- **Plasma NfL + pNfH + GFAP panel** (Feb 2025, PMC11863781): Combined blood panel with strong diagnostic + prognostic accuracy; NfL is the most robust standalone marker
- **Plasma EV-TDP-43 / EV tau ratio** (*Nature Medicine*, 2024): Differentiates ALS from FTD spectrum; detects pathological TDP-43 (present in ~97% of ALS) in blood via exosome isolation
- **Johns Hopkins protein panel blood test** (Aug 2025): >98% accuracy; detects ALS years before symptom onset — not yet in clinical use
- **Brain Chemistry Labs rapid blood assay** (Dec 2025): 97% accuracy using microRNA biomarkers; designed for rapid POC-style deployment
- **Mayo Clinic AI-EMG algorithm** (2025): ML model on needle EMG waveforms identifies denervation patterns invisible to human readers; reduces inter-rater variability
- **Michigan Medicine ML blood model** (Dec 2025): ML applied to routine blood sample + EHR data to flag high-risk patients earlier
- **AI F-wave analysis** (*Brain*, 2025): F-wave response patterns predict ALS from routine NCS data

**Main Limitations:**
- NfL/GFAP are non-specific (elevated in multiple neurodegenerative diseases); require differential context
- EV-TDP-43 assays require ultracentrifugation or size-exclusion chromatography — not yet POC-compatible
- Hopkins/Brain Chemistry Labs tests lack multi-site prospective validation and FDA clearance
- AI-EMG requires specialist-acquired data; doesn't solve the primary care access problem

### 5) Where Healthcare Is Failing (Operational Insight)

**Screening point that drops the ball:**
The **primary care visit** — where patients first present with focal weakness, tripping, or speech changes. PCPs overwhelmingly attribute these to musculoskeletal causes (radiculopathy, rotator cuff, orthopedic injury), initiating orthopedic or spine workups that consume 3–6 months before neurology referral. There is no validated "ALS red flag checklist" embedded in primary care EMR workflows.

**Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:**
**Implementing GCC-based ALS clinical decision support (CDS) alerts in neurology EMR systems.** Many tertiary centers still use El Escorial/Awaji internally. Switching to GCC criteria + adding structured EMG interpretation templates aligned to GCC would allow earlier "probable ALS" classification without waiting for multi-region spread. This is a **protocol + template change** — no new technology required.

**High-risk population missed:**
- **Patients aged 60+** — present later (4+ months before first doctor visit), are more likely to have comorbidities masking ALS symptoms, and are less likely to be referred urgently
- **Black and Hispanic patients** — face longer delays due to differential PCP referral urgency and reduced access to ALS specialty centers
- **Bulbar-onset ALS patients** — often referred to ENT or speech therapy first, delaying neurology entry by 4–8 months
- **Rural patients** — limited access to EMG-capable neurologists; telehealth neurology not yet standard for ALS workup

### 7) First-Principles Signal Hunt (What We Should Measure Earlier)

**Hidden signal candidate:**
- **Presymptomatic plasma NfL trajectory** — NfL rises in a predictable, accelerating curve starting 12–24 months before symptom onset in familial ALS (fALS) carriers. In sporadic ALS (sALS, 90% of cases), the same trajectory likely exists but is undetected because there's no baseline measurement. A **single baseline NfL at age 50** in high-risk individuals (family history, occupational exposure, military veterans — who have 1.5–2× higher ALS risk) would enable trajectory monitoring.
- **EV-TDP-43 ratio in blood** — pathological TDP-43 mislocalization is present in 97% of ALS; its detection in plasma exosomes (Nature Medicine 2024) represents the most disease-specific early signal available. The bottleneck is the extraction method, not the biology.
- **Voice/speech biomarkers** — subtle dysarthria precedes clinical diagnosis by months in bulbar-onset ALS. Smartphone-based speech analysis (sustained vowel phonation, diadochokinesis) could serve as a passive, continuous screening signal — especially relevant for high-risk populations.
- **Wearable motor variability** — fine motor tremor patterns and gait asymmetry detectable via smartwatch/accelerometer data months before clinical weakness threshold is reached.

**Minimal sampling change needed:**
- **Blood** (serum/plasma): NfL + GFAP already available via standard venipuncture on Quest/LabCorp platforms. EV-TDP-43 requires a modified pre-analytical protocol (EDTA tube, rapid centrifugation, -80°C storage) — achievable in any academic lab.
- **No new sample type needed** — the biology is in the blood; the gap is in the ordering protocol and clinical decision integration.

### 10) Pattern Insight (Meta-Learning)

**Recurring diagnostic failure pattern reinforced today:**

ALS continues a pattern seen across multiple high-impact diseases: **the biological signal arrives years before clinical systems are designed to look for it.** The recurring architecture of failure is:

> *Early biomarker exists (NfL, EV-TDP-43) → validated in research cohorts → not integrated into clinical order sets → patients route through wrong specialty first (orthopedics, ENT, psychiatry) → arrive at the right specialist late → gold standard confirms what biology knew 12 months earlier.*

This is the same pattern as:
- **Pancreatic cancer** (CA19-9 rises 2 years before diagnosis; no routine surveillance protocol)
- **Alzheimer's** (p-tau217 detectable 25 years pre-symptom; not yet in primary care workflows)
- **Ovarian cancer** (no approved early screening despite liquid biopsy advances)

**What's different about ALS today (2026):**
ALS is at a **critical inflection point** — multiple high-accuracy blood tests (Hopkins >98%, Brain Chemistry Labs 97%) now exist, GCC criteria are validated, and AI-EMG is operational at Mayo Clinic. The barrier is no longer *biology* — it's **clinical workflow integration and regulatory pathway**. This means the opportunity window for hospital quality improvement and startup plays is *right now*, before a dominant commercial test captures the market.

**Generalizable opportunity forming:**
Across diseases, the pattern suggests a **"biomarker-to-workflow gap"** as the next major frontier — not discovering new biomarkers, but building the clinical infrastructure (reflex order sets, CDS alerts, specialist routing protocols, CLIA lab panels) to deploy the ones we already have. The startup that builds a **"neurodegenerative triage panel"** (NfL + GFAP + p-tau217 + pNfH, reflex-ordered at neurology referral) captures value across ALS, Alzheimer's, and Parkinson's simultaneously — a horizontal diagnostic platform play with enormous clinical utility and a clear reimbursement path.
