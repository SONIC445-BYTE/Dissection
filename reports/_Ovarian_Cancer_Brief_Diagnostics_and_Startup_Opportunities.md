Subject: Daily Early-Diagnosis Brief — Ovarian Cancer (High-Grade Serous Carcinoma) — 2026-03-28

1) Snapshot (one line)
Ovarian Cancer (HGSOC) — why early diagnosis fails in practice: Precursor lesions originate in the fallopian tubes and shed locally down the reproductive tract, evading systemic blood-based detection until late-stage peritoneal metastasis.

2) Why early diagnosis fails (3–5 bullets)
*   **Biological barrier:** High-Grade Serous Ovarian Carcinoma (HGSOC) typically begins as Serous Tubal Intraepithelial Carcinoma (STIC) in the fallopian tubes. Early shedding is luminal (into the uterus/cervix) rather than vascular. Symptoms (bloating, early satiety) are highly non-specific.
*   **Test limitation:** The standard biomarker, CA-125, lacks early-stage sensitivity (misses ~50% of Stage I) and specificity (elevated in benign conditions like endometriosis). Transvaginal ultrasound (TVUS) cannot resolve microscopic tubal precursors.
*   **System failure:** The landmark UKCTOCS trial proved current population screening (CA-125 + TVUS) does not reduce mortality, leading to a policy vacuum. Primary care pathways heavily delay specialist referrals, frequently misdiagnosing early symptoms as IBS or menopause.

3) Detection window & gap (concise)
*   **Earliest detectable signal (research / ideal):** 5–7 years (STIC precursor phase in fallopian tubes, shedding mutant TP53).
*   **Typical clinical detection:** Stage III/IV (widespread peritoneal spread, ascites).
*   **Gap to close:** 3–5 years (Shifting diagnosis from Stage III/IV to Stage I/II increases 5-year survival from <30% to >90%).

4) What’s being used today (gold standard + emergent)
*   **Gold standard(s):** CA-125 blood test, Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVUS), ROMA index (CA-125 + HE4), definitive surgical pathology.
*   **Emerging research / tools:** Proximal fluid multi-omics (e.g., PapSEEK using routine Pap smear fluid), ctDNA methylation profiles, p53 autoantibodies, extracellular vesicle (EV) isolation from uterine lavage.
*   **Main limitations:** Blood ctDNA fraction is extremely low in Stage I. Novel fluid sampling (like uterine lavage) lacks standardized, non-invasive clinical workflows and infrastructure.

5) Where healthcare is failing (operational insight)
*   **Screening point that drops the ball:** Primary care. Vague abdominal symptoms in women >50 rarely trigger immediate gynecologic oncology workups, delaying initial CA-125/TVUS orders by 6–12 months.
*   **Bottleneck most fixable in 90 days:** Lack of automated EMR symptom-clustering algorithms to flag persistent, non-specific GI/pelvic complaints and reflex them to a standardized diagnostic pathway.
*   **High-risk population missed:** BRCA1/2 carriers and Lynch syndrome patients who delay prophylactic bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (surgical menopause) due to a lack of reliable intermediate monitoring tools.
