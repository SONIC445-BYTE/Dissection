### 2) Why Early Diagnosis Fails (3–5 bullets)

- **Biological barrier:** ATTR-CM causes a slowly progressive restrictive cardiomyopathy with non-specific symptoms (dyspnea, fatigue, bilateral carpal tunnel, lumbar stenosis) that are indistinguishable from hypertensive heart disease, HFpEF, or HCM for years before overt heart failure. The amyloid deposition begins 5–10 years before clinical presentation; there is no circulating biomarker with adequate specificity in early disease.

- **Test limitation:** Standard echocardiography — the universal first-line cardiac imaging tool — has a cardiologist sensitivity of only **~47%** for early ATTR-CM. The pathognomonic "apical sparing" pattern on strain imaging is frequently missed or misattributed. The gold standard (technetium-99m PYP/DPD scintigraphy) is a nuclear medicine scan that requires specialist referral and is absent from most community hospital cardiology protocols. Endomyocardial biopsy (histological gold standard) is invasive and rarely performed.

- **System failure:** ATTR-CM is systematically missed in three high-traffic clinical scenarios where it is prevalent: (a) HFpEF clinics — where patients are labelled "diastolic dysfunction" and managed symptomatically; (b) TAVR/aortic stenosis pre-op workup — co-prevalence of ATTR-CM is 6–16% and is almost never screened; (c) Bilateral carpal tunnel syndrome in men >60 — a 5–10 year prodrome of wild-type ATTR-CM that is a missed screening trigger in every orthopedic and hand surgery unit worldwide.

- **Awareness gap:** Wild-type ATTR-CM (wtATTR-CM) affects predominantly men >60 and was considered rare until systematic autopsy and scintigraphy studies revealed prevalence of **13–17% in HFpEF patients** and **up to 25% in men >80** with heart failure. The field has not yet updated clinical pathways to reflect this revised epidemiology.

- **Treatment urgency ignored:** Tafamidis (Vyndamax/Vyndaqel) and the newer acoramidis received regulatory approval with clear mortality benefit — but only in patients with confirmed ATTR-CM. The window to initiate therapy and halt progression is **early-to-mid stage** disease. By the time diagnosis occurs (Stage III, NYHA Class III), much of the benefit window is lost.

### 4) What's Being Used Today (Gold Standard + Emergent)

**Gold Standards:**
- **Technetium-99m PYP/DPD/HMDP Scintigraphy** (nuclear scintigraphy): Non-invasive, Grade 2–3 uptake = ATTR-CM diagnosis without biopsy. Sensitivity ~91%, specificity ~87% when combined with serum/urine immunofixation to exclude AL amyloidosis. **Problem:** Requires nuclear medicine facility, specialist referral, is not in any primary care or general cardiology screening protocol.
- **Endomyocardial biopsy + mass spectrometry typing:** Definitive histological gold standard. Too invasive for screening.
- **Echocardiography (strain imaging, apical sparing pattern):** Widely available but operator-dependent; sensitivity 47% in community settings for early disease.
- **Genetic testing (TTR gene sequencing):** Essential for hereditary ATTR (hATTR) — V122I (African ancestry), V30M (Portuguese/Swedish). Identifies at-risk families but not used in population screening.

**Emerging Research / Tools (2024–2026):**
- **AI-ECG (Anumana/Mayo Clinic algorithm; PMcardio):** AUROC **0.91–0.93** for ATTR-CM detection from standard 12-lead ECG. Sensitivity up to **99% at high recall thresholds** vs. 47% for standard cardiologist interpretation. A 2026 *Heart Rhythm Journal* paper specifically addresses paced-rhythm failure modes in prior AI-ECG models. FDA breakthrough designation pathway underway.
- **AI-Echocardiography (AI-Echo):** Deep learning models trained to detect apical sparing and longitudinal strain patterns automatically — deployed as passive overlay on existing echo systems. Preclinical progression study (ResearchGate, 2024) demonstrated AI-Echo + AI-ECG can detect ATTR-CM **3–5 years before clinical diagnosis** in prospective cohorts.
- **Serum TTR stability assays (fluorescence-based):** Measure kinetic stability of TTR tetramer — low stability = high amyloidogenic risk. Useful for hATTR carrier monitoring; not yet validated for wtATTR screening.
- **Cardiac MRI with T1 mapping + ECV:** Detects diffuse myocardial fibrosis/amyloid infiltration earlier than echo. High sensitivity but expensive, time-consuming, and contraindicated in pacemaker patients.

**Main Limitations:**
- PYP scan: access, cost (~$1,200–$2,500), radiation, no community-level deployment
- AI-ECG: not yet integrated into major EMR systems as a triggered alert; regulatory clearance in progress
- AI-Echo: requires retraining sonographers; vendor-specific, not universally available
- Cardiac MRI: cost, contraindications, throughput

### 7) First-Principles Signal Hunt (What We Should Measure Earlier)

**Hidden signal candidate:**
The **AI-ECG low-voltage + pseudo-infarct pattern** is the single most underutilized early signal. In ATTR-CM, amyloid infiltration produces a paradox: thick walls (high mass) but low ECG voltage — because amyloid is electrically inert. This voltage-mass dissociation is detectable on a standard 12-lead ECG years before symptoms. Additionally, **longitudinal strain "apical sparing" on echo** (base/mid strain worse than apex) is a semi-quantitative marker detectable 2–3 years before overt cardiomyopathy. Neither is routinely computed or flagged.

**Secondary hidden signal:** **Bilateral carpal tunnel + lumbar spinal stenosis + biceps tendon rupture** in men >60 — the "soft tissue amyloid triad" — is a clinical pattern that precedes cardiac involvement by 5–10 years and is never connected to cardiac amyloid risk in any current clinical pathway.

**Minimal sampling change needed:**
- **No new blood draw required.** The ECG is already being done. The echo is already being done. The surgery pre-op assessment is already being done. The gap is purely **algorithmic + workflow** — applying AI to existing data streams and adding a structured reflex referral trigger.
