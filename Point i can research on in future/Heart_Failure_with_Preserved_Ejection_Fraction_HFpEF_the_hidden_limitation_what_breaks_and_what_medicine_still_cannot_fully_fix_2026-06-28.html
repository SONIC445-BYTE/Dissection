TODAY'S TOPIC: Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF)
Specialty: Cardiovascular Medicine

1. ONE-LINE EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
HFpEF is a systemic pro-inflammatory state masquerading as a cardiac-centric pump failure, where macroscopic preservation of ejection fraction conceals a microscopic collapse of myocardial compliance and microvascular density.

2. WHY THIS CONDITION MATTERS
HFpEF now accounts for >50% of all heart failure hospitalizations. Unlike HFrEF (Reduced EF), where we have the "four pillars" of therapy, HFpEF mortality remains stubbornly high. It is the "great masquerader" of geriatrics and metabolic medicine, often dismissed as "just old age" or "deconditioning" until the patient presents with acute pulmonary edema despite a "normal" echocardiogram.

3. THE EXACT PATHWAY / MECHANISM
The pathophysiology follows a "top-down" systemic-to-cardiac insult:

  • Systemic Inflammation: Comorbidities (obesity, DM2, CKD, HTN) trigger a systemic pro-inflammatory state (elevated IL-6, TNF-α, CRP).

  • Coronary Microvascular Endothelial Dysfunction: Pro-inflammatory cytokines induce endothelial cells to produce Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS).

  • NO-sGC-cGMP-PKG Pathway Suppression: ROS (specifically superoxide) reduces Nitric Oxide (NO) bioavailability. This limits the activation of soluble Guanylate Cyclase (sGC), leading to low cyclic Guanylate Monophosphate (cGMP) and low Protein Kinase G (PKG) activity.

  • Titin Hypophosphorylation: PKG normally phosphorylates the giant elastic protein titin (specifically the N2B isoform). Hypophosphorylated titin is rigid, acting like a stiff spring rather than an elastic one.

  • Myofibroblast Activation: TGF-β signaling (via SMAD2/3) converts fibroblasts to myofibroblasts, depositing excessive Type I collagen in the interstitium.


4. THE POINT OF FAILURE
The fundamental failure is ventricular-vascular uncoupling. The heart cannot fill at low pressures. The "point of failure" is the microvascular-interstitial interface. The loss of microvascular density (rarefaction) reduces the oxygen diffusion reserve, meaning the heart fails not because it can't pump, but because it can't "relax" or receive enough oxygen to meet the metabolic demands of diastole.

5. WHY THE SYSTEM BREAKS AT THAT POINT
Evolutionarily, the heart is optimized for "fight or flight" (high adrenergic output). It is not evolved to handle the chronic, low-grade metabolic "simmer" of modern obesity and hyperinsulinemia. The myocardium is highly oxygen-dependent; when the microvasculature rarefies due to chronic inflammation, the energetic cost of sequestering calcium back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum (via SERCA2a) during diastole becomes unsustainable.

6. COMPENSATION AND SUPPRESSION

  • Compensation: The left atrium (LA) undergoes remodeling and enlargement to maintain LV filling. The body increases heart rate to maintain cardiac output (since stroke volume is fixed by stiffness).

  • Suppression: The brain suppresses the sensation of dyspnea initially, leading patients to subconsciously limit activity ("lifestyle pruning").

  • Cost: Elevated LA pressures lead to pulmonary hypertension (Group 2 PH) and eventual right ventricular (RV) failure. By the time the patient feels "sick," the pulmonary vasculature is often irreversibly remodeled.


7. CURRENT CLINICAL WORKAROUNDS
  • SGLT2 Inhibitors (Empagliflozin/Dapagliflozin): Reduce epicardial fat inflammation and improve sodium homeostasis. (The current gold standard).

  • MRAs (Spironolactone): Attempt to limit fibrosis via aldosterone antagonism.

  • ARNI (Sacubitril/Valsartan): Enhances natriuretic peptide levels to counteract RAAS.

  • GLP-1 Receptor Agonists (Semaglutide): Addressing the "upstream" obesity-driven inflammation (STEP-HFpEF trial data).

  • Diuretics: Symptomatic volume management (Loop diuretics).


8. WHY EACH WORKAROUND IS INCOMPLETE
  • SGLT2i: Improve outcomes but do not "cure" the underlying diastolic stiffness; the NNT for mortality is still higher than in HFrEF.

  • MRAs: Limited by hyperkalemia and often started too late to reverse established cross-linked collagen.

  • ARNI: The PARAGON-HF trial showed only marginal benefits, specifically in women and those at the lower end of the "preserved" EF range (45-57%).

  • Diuretics: They reduce preload, which helps pulmonary congestion, but can easily "underfill" a stiff LV, leading to a drop in cardiac output and renal prerenal azotemia (the "cardiorenal" trap).


9. WHAT IS REVERSIBLE VS IRREVERSIBLE
  • Reversible: Myocyte hypertrophy, early-stage titin stiffness (via PKG activation), and fluid overload.

  • Irreversible: Advanced replacement fibrosis (collagen type I scarring), microvascular rarefaction (once vessels are lost, they rarely regrow in the same architecture), and advanced LA remodeling/Atrial Fibrillation.

  • Window: There is a "pre-HFpEF" window where aggressive metabolic control can prevent the transition, but once PCWP is elevated at rest, the "stiffness" is largely structural.


10. DIAGNOSTIC BLIND SPOTS
  • The Resting Echo: A patient can have a "normal" EF (60%) and "normal" filling pressures at rest, yet have a 4-fold increase in PCWP the moment they walk.

  • The BNP Paradox: Obese patients (the majority of HFpEF) have lower BNP/NT-proBNP levels due to increased clearance receptors on adipocytes, leading to false negatives.

  • The H2FPEF Score: Clinicians often ignore this, relying solely on imaging rather than clinical probability (Heavy, Hypertensive, Atrial Fibrillation, Pulmonary Hypertension, Elder, Filling Pressures).


11. CURRENT RESEARCH DIRECTIONS
  • Inorganic Nitrates: Attempting to bypass the NO-sGC pathway (Mixed results in NEAT-HFpEF).

  • Anti-inflammatory biologics: Targeting IL-1β or IL-6 directly.

  • AI-ECG: Using deep learning to detect "silent" diastolic dysfunction from a standard 12-lead ECG (Experimental - high accuracy in Mayo Clinic models).

  • Interatrial Shunt Devices: Creating a "controlled" ASD to decompress the LA (REDUCE LAP-HF II showed negative results, but sub-phenotyping continues).

13. STARTUP / PRODUCT / AI / RESEARCH OPPORTUNITY IDEAS
  • Continuous PCWP Monitoring: A "CardioMEMS" for the masses—non-invasive or minimally invasive ways to track LA pressure during daily life.

  • Titin-Targeting Small Molecules: Drugs that specifically increase titin phosphorylation without systemic vasodilator side effects.

  • AI-Phenotyping Tool: A software layer for Echo machines that automatically calculates the H2FPEF score and suggests "Stress Echo" for indeterminate cases.


14. WHAT A FUTURE CLINICIAN-SCIENTIST SHOULD NOTICE
When you see a patient with obesity, hypertension, and "shortness of breath on exertion" whose Echo says "Normal EF," do not stop there. Look at the E/e' ratio, look at the Left Atrial Volume Index (LAVI), and if the BNP is "borderline," remember the obesity suppression effect. The "normal" EF is a trap; the real pathology is in the reserve.

15. KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • HFpEF is a systemic inflammatory disease, not just a heart disease.

  • Ejection Fraction is a measure of chamber shrinkage, not a measure of contractility or health.

  • Titin stiffness and collagen fibrosis are the dual mechanical enemies.

  • SGLT2 inhibitors are the first true breakthrough, but they are not a cure.

  • Diagnostic gold standard remains invasive hemodynamics with exercise, though rarely performed.
