# 2. PORTER'S FIVE FORCES

## Force 1 — Threat of New Entrants: **HIGH ⬆️**

**Lowering barriers 🟢:**
- Foundation model APIs mean a competent team can ship a credible AI nutrition coach in weeks. Healthify's own AI is rented from a vendor anyone can rent from.
- Photo-calorie recognition is now a commodity capability (GPT-4V-class models do it out of the box).
- Distribution via app stores is open.
- Observed in the wild: FitTrack AI, NutriScan, Nutrola, Cal AI, Kint Health — 🟢 several appear in Reddit threads about Healthify alternatives, and one was built by a disgruntled Healthify Rx user.

**Remaining barriers 🟡:**
- The Indian food catalogue depth (14 years, NIN collaboration).
- The coach network and its cost structure.
- Brand and SEO estate.
- Clinical/pharma relationships for the Rx product.

**Verdict:** 🟡 Barriers to *entry* have collapsed; barriers to *scale in India* remain meaningful. In the US, Healthify is itself the new entrant with no barriers protecting it.

## Force 2 — Bargaining Power of Suppliers: **HIGH ⬆️**

- **OpenAI 🟢:** the single most powerful supplier. Sole provider for Snap, Ria and Copilot with no evidenced fallback. Controls price, capability, availability, and policy. **Inference cost rises with the very engagement Healthify optimises for.**
- **Abbott 🟢:** sole CGM supplier.
- **Tata 1mg 🟢:** sole Rx fulfilment channel — and a potential competitor with pharmacy, diagnostics and the Tata brand.
- **Novo Nordisk / Eli Lilly 🟢:** control the drug that underpins Healthify's growth thesis.
- **Apple / Google 🟢:** control distribution, take 15–30% of IAP revenue, and control the photo permissions Auto Snap depends on.
- **Coaches 🟡:** individually low power (abundant Indian supply), but collectively the constraint on service quality.

**Verdict:** 🟡 **Healthify is unusually supplier-exposed for a software company.** It rents its intelligence, its sensor, its drug, its pharmacy and its distribution.

## Force 3 — Bargaining Power of Buyers: **HIGH ⬆️**

- 🟢 Switching costs are low for the free tier and moderate for paid (log history, coach relationship).
- 🟢 Abundant substitutes, many free.
- 🟢 Indian consumers are extremely price-sensitive — Healthify's own pricing philosophy concedes this.
- 🟢 Buyers actively organise: complaint platforms, Reddit threads, chargebacks, an entire "HealthifyMe alternative" content ecosystem.
- 🟢 Healthify's response — minimum contracts, refund refusal, one-switch coach limits — is **the behaviour of a company with weak voluntary retention.**
- 🟡 In B2B, corporate buyers have very high power (multiple vendors, annual RFPs) and Healthify cannot meet US enterprise compliance requirements.

**Verdict:** 🟢 High and rising.

## Force 4 — Threat of Substitutes: **VERY HIGH ⬆️⬆️**

| Substitute | Threat |
|---|---|
| **ChatGPT / general AI** | 🟢 **Existential.** Users publicly substituting: *"better ask chatgpt — quicker, free and efficient."* |
| Free calorie trackers | 🟢 High |
| Free AI photo-cal apps | 🟢 High |
| Apple Health / Health Connect | 🟢 High — OS-level, free, improving |
| Human dietitian (in person) | 🟡 Medium — expensive but higher trust |
| GLP-1 drugs alone (no programme) | 🟢 High — the drug does the work; the companion is optional |
| Hospital GLP-1 programmes | 🟢 Rising — Apollo, Max, Narayana all launched |
| Gyms, YouTube, Instagram coaches | 🟡 Medium |
| Wearables with coaching (Whoop, Oura, Ultrahuman) | 🟡 Medium-High |

**Verdict:** 🟢 **The highest of the five forces.** The core value proposition — "an AI that answers nutrition questions" — is now available free from multiple general-purpose providers.

## Force 5 — Competitive Rivalry: **HIGH ⬆️**

- **India:** Cult.fit, Fittr, Elevate Now, Sugarfit, BeatO, Ultrahuman, Apollo 24/7, Tata 1mg, VLCC, FITPASS + hospital chains. 🟢 GLP-1 has drawn everyone into the same arena.
- **US:** Noom, MyFitnessPal, WeightWatchers (post-bankruptcy), Hims & Hers, Ro, Omada, Virta, Found, Calibrate, Levels, Function, Superpower.
- **Price competition:** 🟢 Intense. Healthify at ₹208/mo; free alternatives below that; and W Health Ventures notes GLP-1 programme pricing will see "aggressive price-based competition."
- **Exit barriers:** 🟡 High for Healthify — 600+ coaches, brand commitments, investor expectations.

**Verdict:** 🟡 High and intensifying, with the GLP-1 wave collapsing previously separate categories into one battlefield.

## Five Forces Summary

| Force | Intensity | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Threat of new entrants | **HIGH** | ⬆️ Rising |
| Supplier power | **HIGH** | ⬆️ Rising |
| Buyer power | **HIGH** | ⬆️ Rising |
| Threat of substitutes | **VERY HIGH** | ⬆️⬆️ Rising fast |
| Competitive rivalry | **HIGH** | ⬆️ Rising |

> 🟡 **Industry attractiveness verdict: POOR AND DETERIORATING.** All five forces are unfavourable and all five are worsening. Structural profitability in consumer AI nutrition is being competed away.
>
> **Ovexis implication:** *do not enter this industry as defined.* Enter the adjacent, structurally more attractive industry — **longitudinal clinical intelligence** — where substitutes are weak (no free equivalent), buyer switching costs are high (your medical record), supplier power is diffuse (many labs, many models), and barriers to entry are high (compliance, clinical credibility, interoperability engineering).

## Risk Concentration Analysis 🟡
Three of the top five risks (R01, R06, R07) are **structural, not operational** — meaning they cannot be fixed by working harder. They require a change in what the company *is*:
- R01 demands moving up the value stack beyond AI chat.
- R06 demands abandoning the 300:1 unit economics.
- R07 demands a new acquisition model.

> 🟡 **Each of these three fixes contradicts the cost-leadership strategy that made Healthify successful.** That is the definition of an innovator's dilemma — and it is precisely the gap Ovexis should walk through.
