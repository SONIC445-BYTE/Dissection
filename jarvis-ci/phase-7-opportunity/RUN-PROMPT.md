## Deliverable: `OPPORTUNITY-MAP.md`

### 1. Reality reconciliation
Place the **verified** capability ledger against the ecosystem map. Not the roadmap — the ledger. What exists, at what stage, in which layer.

Carry forward the Phase R states honestly: **VERIFIED · BUILT · WIRED · DARK · DESIGNED · ABSENT.**

> The DARK category deserves specific attention. Built-but-unwired capability is inventory, not output. Phase R found several instances. Wiring existing dark capability is almost always cheaper than building new capability, and it should be evaluated before anything else on the roadmap.

### 2. Gap analysis — three kinds, treated differently

| Gap type | Definition | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Commodity gap** | Missing something the ecosystem gives away free | Integrate immediately. Never build. |
| **Contested gap** | Missing something nobody has solved | Evaluate: hard or neglected? (Phase 6 §6) |
| **Moat gap** | Missing something that would compound | **Build — this is the roadmap** |

Misclassifying a commodity gap as a moat gap is how teams waste quarters rebuilding Playwright.

### 3. Thesis verdicts — final

### 4. Layer posture — final
Confirm or revise every posture in the baseline §2 table, now against evidence rather than intention. Every revision needs a trigger and a Decision Ledger entry.

### 5. Opportunity ranking

| Opportunity | Evidence | Effort | Moat class | Unlocks | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rank by **(moat durability × market access) / effort**. Not by interest. Not by tractability.

> Phase R found investment flowing toward layers with satisfying engineering problems while mission-critical layers stayed empty. Ranking by durability-over-effort is the countermeasure to that drift.

### 6. Sequencing
What must come first? What is blocked by what? What is genuinely parallel?

**Explicitly evaluate: what should be *stopped*?** Sequencing is as much about subtraction as addition.

### 7. Roadmap
Grounded in the actual stage, not the aspirational one. Every item traceable to a corpus finding.

### 8. Architecture decisions
What must change structurally? Each with the evidence that forces it.

### 9. Adapter strategy
Per `05-healthcare-ontology.md` §VIII.2 ladder. Which systems, which rank, which order — informed by Phase 5's real integration costs.

### 10. Developer ecosystem
SDK · plugins · marketplace · community. Only if Phase 3 L13 and Phase 6 support it. **If the evidence says the ecosystem play is premature, say so.**

### 11. The validation question ⭐
Phase R's central finding was a **validation gap**: no point in the plan where a clinician touches the system before three stages are built.

Answer concretely: **what is the smallest thing that puts a real user in front of the system, and when?**

Everything else in this phase is subordinate to that answer. A perfect roadmap validated by nobody is the Forward Health failure mode, and the corpus contains that post-mortem for exactly this reason.

## Discipline

- Every recommendation cites corpus evidence — dossier claim IDs, layer findings, technology classifications.
- Every recommendation states what would make it wrong.
- **Recommendations that violate a stated non-goal must either be rejected or argue the non-goal is wrong.** No silent scope expansion.
- Stage-honest throughout. A roadmap assuming S3 when reality is S1 cannot execute.
