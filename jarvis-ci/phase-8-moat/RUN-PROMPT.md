## The question

**How does JARVIS become impossible to replace?**

Not "how does it become good." Good is table stakes and gets commoditised. This phase is about **structural** advantage — the kind that survives a well-funded competitor deciding to copy you.

## Deliverable: `MOAT-ENGINEERING.md`

### 1. Moat inventory — what exists today
From the capability ledger, honestly assessed. Phase R identified genuine seeds:

- **Honest-failure discipline** — consistently applied, CONTESTED-underserved ecosystem-wide
- **Approval gates + rollback** — snapshot-first, reverts on partial failure, brutal to retrofit
- **Commit-message rigour** — records what was *not* done; makes the codebase auditable

> These three are unglamorous and that is precisely why they are defensible. Fast-moving generic agent companies skip them because demos don't reward them. In a regulated context they are the licence to operate.

Score each 0–5 with evidence. Distinguish **seed** from **moat** — a seed is a property that *could* compound; a moat already does.

### 3. Compounding mechanics ⭐
A moat that does not compound is a **head start**, and head starts expire.

For each proposed moat: what makes it *stronger* with usage? If nothing, it is a feature, not a moat — say so and demote it.

### 4. Copy-resistance analysis
For each moat, answer honestly: **if a well-funded competitor decided to copy this tomorrow, how long would it take?**

| Resistance | Time to copy | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Weak | < 6 months | Features, UX, integrations |
| Moderate | 6–18 months | Deep engineering, accumulated ops knowledge |
| Strong | 18 months+ | Regulatory posture, clinical relationships, accumulated workflow data |
| Structural | Effectively never | Network effects, national infrastructure position, data impossible to reassemble |

**Anything under 6 months is not a moat.** Be ruthless here — the temptation is to score your own work as harder to copy than it is.

### 5. Moat sequencing
Which moats must be built first? Which depend on others? Which need scale that doesn't exist yet?

Regulatory and workflow moats are slow — start early. Network effects need users — cannot be started early. Sequence accordingly.

### 6. Anti-moats
What is being built that **looks** like a moat but isn't?

Phase 6 §9 distinguishes *durable* from *rented* moats. Distribution deals, platform placement, and vendor exclusivity read like owned moats and are rented. Apply that lens to JARVIS's own plans.

### 7. The switching-cost design question
What would a customer lose by leaving JARVIS after 12 months? After 3 years?

If the answer is "not much," the product has no retention architecture — and retention architecture must be designed deliberately, not hoped for.

### 8. Moat vs. mission tension
Some moats are hostile to users — lock-in, data hostage-taking, deliberate non-portability.

**Name the ones JARVIS refuses to build, and why.** A stated refusal is itself a trust asset in healthcare, where data portability is both a regulatory direction and an ethical expectation.
